# E3 Jurong Island Candidate-Context, Siting, Cooling and Infrastructure Feasibility

## 1. Decision

**Jurong Island is a credible candidate industrial-integration context but is NOT DEMONSTRATED as a feasible nuclear site.**

Its advantages are unusually strong for the chemical side of the project: a ~3,000 ha integrated energy-and-chemicals island, more than 100 companies, more than 100 km of pipelines, port/logistics access, natural-gas/LNG infrastructure, industrial water, seawater access, active hydrogen/CCUS projects and nearly 300 ha reserved island-wide for new-energy solutions.

Those advantages do not establish nuclear suitability. The same island contains refineries, chemical plants, hazardous-material storage/pipelines and intense marine activity. IAEA siting standards explicitly require such human-induced hazards, cooling-water availability, external events, population/emergency-response feasibility and site characteristics to be evaluated. Singapore has not selected a nuclear site or decided to deploy nuclear energy.

**E3 classification: JURONG ISLAND = CONDITIONAL CANDIDATE CONTEXT / NUCLEAR SITE SUITABILITY UNRESOLVED.**

## 2. Current Jurong Island context

JTC states that Jurong Island is approximately 3,000 ha, contains over 100 global energy and chemicals companies and more than 100 km of pipelines. Close to 300 ha has been set aside for new-energy solutions. This supports examination of Jurong because the project requires methane, high-temperature process integration, hydrogen handling, CO2 conditioning/export, industrial utilities and coastal logistics.

Relevant current developments include:
- hydrogen-ready gas generation and ammonia-power/bunkering studies;
- Aster/Air Liquide collaboration on low-carbon hydrogen with integrated carbon capture;
- industrial hydrogen movements within Singapore's integrated E&C ecosystem;
- district-energy/shared-cooling and thermal-grid studies;
- low-carbon testbeds and pilot infrastructure.

None of these projects is evidence of a nuclear parcel, nuclear licence or nuclear-ready industrial separation distance.

### LCT3

A*STAR's S$62.5m Low Carbon Technology Translational Testbed (LCT3) is being established on Jurong Island for pilot-scale testing and scale-up of **carbon capture/utilisation and low-carbon hydrogen technologies**, including high-pressure/high-temperature industrial operation and modular plug-and-play equipment.

**LCT3 is not a nuclear project, reactor testbed, nuclear site or nuclear approval.** Its relevance is limited to demonstrating that Jurong is an active industrial decarbonisation/testbed environment.

## 4. Land and footprint screen

A defensible total site area cannot yet be calculated because equipment footprint, plant plot, security/protected area and emergency-planning area are different quantities.

Source/analog anchors:

1. **IAEA/JAEA GTHTR300 catalogue:** approximately 250 m x 250 m = **6.25 ha** plant footprint for a **four-reactor plant**. This is not a one-reactor site area and cannot be divided by four to infer a licensed parcel.
2. **JAEA HTTR:** 30 MWth test-reactor site ~50,000 m2 = **5 ha**; reactor building ~48 x 50 m. This demonstrates why reactor-building footprint cannot be equated to total nuclear site area; it is not scalable to 600 MWth.
3. **External SMR+CCS technical study:** a 100,000 Nm3/h H2 SMR+CCS plant was estimated at ~110 x 150 m = **1.65 ha** process footprint. The project's 130 MMSCFD train is larger (~153,000 Nm3/h by simple standard-volume conversion), and there are two trains. Even treating 1.65 ha per train only as a lower-order analog gives >3.3 ha of chemical-process plot before scaling, utilities, separation, CO2/H2 buffering, roads or nuclear security. This is not a project footprint.

Jurong's 3,000 ha island area and ~300 ha new-energy reservation show island-scale land availability, but **do not demonstrate a nuclear-compatible parcel**. E3 cannot defensibly sum the heterogeneous source footprints into a total nuclear project area.

Required next evidence:
- parcel-level land envelope;
- final reactor/auxiliary layout;
- two-train plot plan;
- cooling-system footprint;
- QRA/PRA-derived separation;
- security/protected area;
- emergency-planning feasibility;
- coastal/flood protection;
- construction/maintenance laydown and logistics.

## 5. Heat-rejection boundary

The preferred architecture has:
[
600-353.6=246.4 {m MWth}
]
of **remaining installed reactor thermal capacity at full reactor rating**.

**246.4 MWth is not the project cooling duty.**

The actual operational heat-rejection duty depends on the chosen operating architecture:
- if reactor power follows the 353.6 MWth process demand, the 246.4 MWth is simply unused installed capacity, not continuously generated heat;
- if the reactor operates at 600 MWth, the remaining 246.4 MWth requires a real sink: power conversion, another process user, dump/heat-rejection system, or another validated use;
- the chemical trains themselves reject lower-grade process heat, and CCS/compression adds cooling demand, but the current INL source record does not expose a complete two-train cooling-duty ledger;
- nuclear decay heat and safety ultimate-heat-sink requirements are separate safety functions and are not sized by this steady-state screen.

Therefore:
**exact normal-operation Q_reject = UNRESOLVED pending a complete reactor operating point + chemical/CCS heat balance.**

## 7. Cooling options

### Once-through seawater — CONDITIONAL
PUB explicitly identifies once-through seawater cooling as an option for Jurong/seafront companies.

Advantages:
- high heat-removal capacity;
- low cooling-tower land demand;
- low freshwater demand;
- established Singapore industrial practice.

Constraints:
- large intake/outfall and pumping infrastructure;
- marine impingement/entrainment, fouling and blockage;
- thermal plume and chemical-treatment effects;
- corrosion;
- coastal flood/storm-surge interface;
- nuclear ultimate-heat-sink reliability/common-cause assessment.

### Seawater cooling tower — CONDITIONAL
PUB identifies seawater cooling towers as technically feasible and economically viable, particularly for greenfield applications.

Advantages:
- lower once-through seawater throughput;
- potentially different outfall/plume burden.

Constraints:
- tower/pond/blowdown footprint;
- salt drift and corrosion;
- fan/pump power;
- plume;
- blowdown treatment/discharge;
- safety/reliability qualification.

### Closed-loop freshwater/industrial-water tower — CONDITIONAL
Singapore has industrial water and desalination capability, including a Jurong Island desalination plant. This makes makeup-water supply physically plausible.

However, using valuable freshwater/industrial water for large-scale heat rejection creates supply/resilience/opportunity-cost questions. No project makeup rate is available.

### Hybrid/dry cooling — FUTURE OPTION
IAEA's GTHTR300 catalogue notes dry cooling can be feasible for the source direct gas-turbine cycle because heat is rejected at relatively high temperature. The present project is a different heat-only/process architecture; dry/hybrid cooling therefore requires a dedicated cycle design and Singapore ambient-performance study.

### Waste-heat use — FUTURE OPTION
JTC/Keppel are studying shared cooling, thermal storage and waste-heat utilisation on Jurong Island. No compatible customer/demand is demonstrated for this project, so E3 applies no heat-use credit.

## 8. Water and steam feasibility

Jurong Island has established industrial-water infrastructure; PUB also confirms the Jurong Island Desalination Plant is part of Singapore's desalination system. This supports **physical plausibility of industrial/process water supply**, not guaranteed project capacity.

The project does not yet have a closed two-train water balance. The canonical source specifies S/C=3.0, but natural-gas composition, condensate recovery, boiler blowdown, capture solvent water, cooling makeup and steam-network integration are insufficiently resolved to convert this into a defensible net freshwater withdrawal.

An existing IEAGHG reference ledger reports 95.3 t/h HP process steam and 46.1 t/h export steam at its 100,000 Nm3/h H2 scale, but that is a different flowsheet and is retained only as an external service-scale analog, not scaled into a project water demand.

**Water/steam status: CONDITIONAL — infrastructure exists; project balance and allocated capacity are unresolved.**

## 9. Natural gas

Two trains require:
- **68 MMSCFD operating NG**;
- approximately **21.10 Bscf/y** at 85% availability.

Jurong Island is connected to Singapore's gas/LNG energy system; EMA notes Singapore receives pipeline gas from Malaysia/Indonesia and LNG through the Jurong Island LNG terminal. The island also hosts extensive gas-fired generation.

This establishes physical plausibility, not a firm 68 MMSCFD project entitlement. Required confirmation:
- connection pressure/location;
- firm capacity;
- redundancy;
- curtailment priority;
- upstream methane/lifecycle performance;
- common-cause dependence on imported gas.

The pathway remains methane-dependent even with nuclear reformer heat.

## 10. Hydrogen handling/offtake

Two trains produce:
- **260 MMSCFD H2**;
- **~195,892 t/y** at 85% availability.

Jurong has real hydrogen/low-carbon-hydrogen activity. JTC/EDB describe the Aster/Air Liquide low-carbon-H2 collaboration, and EDB documents industrial hydrogen movement by pipeline within Singapore's E&C ecosystem.

That does not establish an offtaker for ~196 kt/y. E3 therefore requires:
- aggregate industrial demand/offtake study;
- product pressure/purity/network specification;
- pipeline capacity/routing;
- buffer/storage philosophy;
- curtailment/turndown strategy;
- export assessment only if a real export route is proposed.

**H2 offtake status: CONDITIONAL / CAPACITY NOT SECURED.**

## 11. CO2 logistics and cross-border storage

Two trains produce captured CO2 at approximately:
- **3,496 t/day while operating**;
- **1.085 Mt/y** at 85% availability.

The chain requires:
capture → dehydration/conditioning → compression/liquefaction as selected → buffer → port/pipeline interface → cross-border transport → injection/storage → monitoring/accounting/liability.

Singapore and Indonesia signed a CCS MOU in June 2025, and Singapore is developing cross-border CCS relationships. This is policy/institutional progress, **not an operating storage service**.

Jurong's port/terminal context makes ship export physically plausible, but shipping also adds nuclear external-hazard considerations.

Required gate:
**contracted ≥1.085 MtCO2/y transport-and-permanent-storage capacity with pressure/specification, buffer, loading, schedule, monitoring, liability and carbon-accounting terms.**

## 12. Nuclear/chemical separation

No universal separation distance is adopted.

JAEA has explicitly studied GTHTR300C reactor/hydrogen-plant separation using hydrogen dispersion and blast-overpressure analysis. Singapore separately requires QRA for hazardous installations and pipelines.

For this project the distance must be determined by integrated:
- H2/NG release and explosion modelling;
- CO/toxic/asphyxiant releases;
- CO2 release;
- fire radiation;
- missiles/projectiles;
- nuclear PRA/source term;
- common utility failures;
- prevailing/site meteorology;
- physical-protection/security needs;
- emergency access.

**Nuclear/chemical separation: UNRESOLVED — QRA/PRA/consequence-derived, not a generic radius.**

## 13. External hazards requiring site evaluation

Jurong's candidate context requires explicit screening/characterisation of:

### Human-induced
- refinery/chemical fire and explosion;
- hazardous/toxic releases;
- pipelines and storage;
- port/shipping collision/explosion;
- dangerous-goods transport;
- aircraft/transport where applicable;
- grid/common-utility loss;
- electromagnetic/interference where relevant;
- malicious/security events at high level.

IAEA SSR-1/SSG-79 explicitly require evaluation of nearby land/sea/air transport, industrial fire/explosion/missiles and hazardous-gas releases.

### Natural/coastal
- coastal flooding;
- storm surge/high tide;
- extreme rainfall/compound flooding;
- sea-level rise over plant lifetime;
- waves/tides;
- erosion/sedimentation;
- intake blockage/debris/marine organisms;
- extreme wind/lightning;
- seismic/geotechnical/liquefaction basis.

Singapore's Jurong Island coastal-protection study is active. Government sources state the island study is being coordinated with stakeholders and that waterfront access/business continuity are explicit constraints. National climate projections include up to 1.15 m mean sea-level rise by 2100, with much larger combined extreme-water levels possible.

These are design inputs, not proof Jurong is unsuitable; they are also not closed.

## 14. Nuclear regulatory and siting status

As of 2026:
- Singapore **has not decided to deploy nuclear energy**;
- Government is seriously studying advanced nuclear options;
- IAEA INIR Phase 1 is planned starting in **2027** to assess Singapore's ability to make an informed decision;
- NEA's RPNSG is the national radiation-protection authority and administers the Radiation Protection Act;
- NEA has created a Nuclear Safety Division and is expanding regulator-to-regulator cooperation/training, including with the US NRC, UK ONR and Indonesia BAPETEN;
- existing radiation/safeguards capability does **not** demonstrate a complete power-reactor licensing/siting framework.

MTI has explicitly stated that it is premature to discuss specific nuclear sites while no deployment decision exists.

Therefore:
**Jurong Island is not selected, reserved, licensed or approved for nuclear deployment.**

A future project requires a national decision plus a power-reactor legal/regulatory framework, independent regulatory capability, site survey/evaluation, environmental/radiological assessment, security, safeguards, waste and emergency-planning arrangements.

## 16. Implementation roadmap gates

1. **National nuclear decision/framework gate:** Singapore establishes a decision pathway and power-reactor siting/licensing framework after capability/INIR work.
2. **Candidate parcel gate:** identify a parcel/envelope capable of reactor, two process trains, utilities, security, construction/maintenance access and separation.
3. **External-hazard gate:** SSR-1/SSG-79-compatible survey shows industrial/marine/natural hazards can be bounded by design/protection.
4. **Separation gate:** integrated nuclear/chemical QRA/PRA establishes acceptable layout and propagation limits.
5. **Cooling-duty gate:** close the reactor operating point plus chemical/CCS heat balance; identify normal and safety heat sinks.
6. **Cooling-environment gate:** intake/outfall/tower design, thermal plume, ecology, fouling/blockage, corrosion and coastal protection are acceptable.
7. **Water gate:** close two-train steam/makeup/cooling water balance and secure supply capacity.
8. **NG gate:** secure firm 68 MMSCFD connection/capacity with resilience analysis.
9. **H2 gate:** secure ~195,892 t/y offtake/network/storage/turndown capacity.
10. **CCS gate:** secure ≥1.085 Mt/y cross-border transport/storage with buffer, loading, monitoring, liability and accounting.
11. **Security/EPR gate:** demonstrate nuclear physical protection, emergency access and regulator-approved emergency arrangements.
12. **Site decision gate:** only after the above can Jurong be compared formally against alternative Singapore contexts.

No calendar dates are assigned.

## 17. CN4252 relevance

### Solution-at-scale within Singapore
The production plant can conceptually be located in Singapore while geological CO2 storage occurs cross-border. "Within Singapore" therefore means the decarbonisation plant, hydrogen production and principal industrial integration are domestic; permanent geological storage need not be domestic if a legally/accountably valid cross-border CCS chain exists.

### Feasibility
Jurong materially strengthens process-integration plausibility because relevant industrial, gas, water, hydrogen, port and low-carbon infrastructure exists. Nuclear-site feasibility remains unresolved.

### Potential effectiveness
E2B's two-train scale is physically meaningful in an industrial cluster: ~196 ktH2/y and ~1.085 Mt/y captured CO2 are large infrastructure flows that require real networks/contracts.

### Implementation roadmap
E3 converts "siting/cooling" from a generic limitation into measurable gates: parcel, hazard, separation, cooling, water, NG, H2, CCS, regulatory and emergency/security closure.

### Accuracy
E3 prevents four misleading claims:
- Jurong is not a selected nuclear site;
- LCT3 is not a nuclear project;
- 246.4 MWth is not automatically cooling duty;
- regional CCS agreements are not an operating storage chain.

## 21. E3 closure

**WHY JURONG?** Because it is Singapore's strongest existing energy/chemicals integration context with gas, water, pipelines, port access, hydrogen/CCUS activity and coastal cooling options.

**WHAT PREVENTS A SITE FEASIBILITY CLAIM?** No parcel, nuclear deployment decision, power-reactor licensing framework, external-hazard survey, QRA/PRA separation, emergency/security plan, closed cooling design, H2 offtake or contracted CCS chain.

**CAN COOLING BE QUANTIFIED?** Only as bounded illustrative heat-sink envelopes today. At the Singapore 8 K once-through reference, a 246.4 MWth full-power residual-disposition envelope corresponds to ~7.53 m3/s seawater, while a full 600 MWth ultimate-sink envelope is ~18.34 m3/s. Neither is the canonical project cooling duty.

**E3 final classification: CONDITIONAL CANDIDATE INDUSTRIAL CONTEXT; NUCLEAR SITE FEASIBILITY NOT DEMONSTRATED.**

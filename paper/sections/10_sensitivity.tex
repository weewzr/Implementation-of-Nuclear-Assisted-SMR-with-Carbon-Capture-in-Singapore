\section{Sensitivity and uncertainty}\label{sec:sensitivity}
The main question is whether the preferred two-train result survives changes in the assumptions that matter most. For the incremental cost ratio,
\[
AC=\frac{\Delta C_y}{A_y},
\]
where $\Delta C_y$ is represented incremental annual cost (S\$/y) and $A_y>0$ is annual lifecycle avoidance (tCO$_2$e/y), a larger cost difference raises abatement cost and a larger avoided-emissions denominator reduces it, with other quantities fixed.

The E2B model gives approximately S\$137.74/tCO$_2$e at FOAK (FAIL), S\$74.14/tCO$_2$e at early-commercial/BOAK (projected), and S\$42.84/tCO$_2$e at mature 10-OAK (projected). These values are maturity scenarios, not a statistical uncertainty interval. The model assumes 85\% integrated service availability and two identical Case-6 trains. Real cost and abatement depend on joint reactor/IHX/train/CCS availability, construction learning, site and licensing expenditure, upstream methane emissions, capture performance and contracted CO$_2$ transport/storage.

The older one-train availability, gas-backup and CCS-delivery sensitivity curves are retained in Appendix~\ref{app:historical-sensitivity}. They establish how that earlier model responded to its assumptions, but cannot be directly transferred to the preferred two-train configuration without recalculating fixed costs, shared outages and partial-train operation. In particular, neither the historical low-cost backup case nor its abatement threshold should be presented as a current two-train prediction.

The strongest present conclusion is therefore conditional: the preferred architecture has a projected mature numerical pass but an adverse FOAK result, and the missing project-specific cost, safety and infrastructure data may change the deployment decision.

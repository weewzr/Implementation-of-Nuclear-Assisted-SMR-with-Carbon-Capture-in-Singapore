\section{Techno-economic analysis}\label{sec:tea}
\subsection{Complete-cost scope and the evidence gate}
The cost equation is straightforward, but a credible number requires all the important terms:
\[
AC_{\mathrm{full}}=
\frac{C_{\mathrm{nuclear\ assisted},y}-C_{\mathrm{baseline},y}}
{E_{\mathrm{baseline},y}-E_{\mathrm{nuclear\ assisted},y}}
\quad[\mathrm{S\$/tCO_2e}].
\]
Both annual costs must cover matched hydrogen service and comparable equipment boundaries. Required categories include reformer/furnace or nuclear-heated reformer, WGS, PSA, feed treatment, steam/heat recovery, utilities, catalyst/maintenance, capture absorber/stripper and regeneration, solvent OPEX, CO$_2$ drying/compression and storage, nuclear plant/IHX/secondary loop, and project EPC/owner/site/financing/licensing/waste costs. The previous E2B ledger below represents only selected categories and is \emph{not} a complete chemical-plant estimate. Missing required costs are not zero. Until source-backed bounds are available, a fully costed FOAK, BOAK or mature abatement cost is \textbf{not calculable}. In addition, the denominator remains conditional on PSA tail-gas elemental carbon closure (Section~\ref{sec:carbon}).

The older FOAK S\$137.74/t, BOAK S\$74.14/t and 10-OAK S\$42.84/t values are retained as \emph{represented-scope historical model scenarios}, not verified full-project economic passes. See \texttt{results/FULL\_CHEMICAL\_AND\_CCS\_COST\_LEDGER.md} and \texttt{results/REVISED\_CARBON\_ECONOMICS\_COMPARISON.md}.

\subsection{Start with the whole annual-cost equation}
The economic question is simple: \emph{how much more does it cost each year to avoid one tonne of lifecycle greenhouse-gas emissions?} We first compare two plants providing the same hydrogen service, and only then divide by avoided emissions:
\begin{align}
\Delta C_y &= C_{\mathrm{candidate},y}-C_{\mathrm{baseline},y},\\
A_y &= E_{\mathrm{baseline},y}-E_{\mathrm{candidate},y},\\
AC &= \frac{\Delta C_y}{A_y}.
\end{align}
Here $C$ is annual represented cost (S\$/y), $E$ is annual lifecycle emissions (tCO$_2$e/y), $A_y$ is avoided lifecycle emissions (tCO$_2$e/y), and $AC$ is incremental abatement cost (S\$/tCO$_2$e). Positive $\Delta C_y$ means the candidate costs more; a positive $A_y$ means it emits less. The same hydrogen output and annual operating-time assumption must apply to both sides.

For the \textbf{controlling E2B two-train model}, the actual Rust ledger is
\begin{align}
C_{\mathrm{baseline},y}&=2C_{\mathrm{NG,baseline,train}},\\
C_{\mathrm{candidate},y}
&=2(C_{\mathrm{NG,candidate,train}}+
 C_{\mathrm{CCS,annual,train}}+
 C_{\mathrm{T\&S,train}})\\
&\quad+C_{\mathrm{nuclear,annual}}+
 2C_{\mathrm{IHX/secondary,annual,train}}.
\end{align}
The baseline ledger represents natural-gas expenditure; it does \emph{not} separately add an SMR capital/O\&M charge. The candidate similarly does \emph{not} separately add an unmodelled SMR plant capital charge. The CCS term is the represented annualized capture capital; the nuclear term combines annualized overnight capital and annual operating expense; the IHX/secondary term is a represented per-train interface allowance. These definitions follow \texttt{model/src/e2b\_maturation.rs} and are a deliberately incomplete project-cost boundary, not a claim that all real-world SMR costs are zero.

\subsection{What is not fully priced here?}
The historical one-train differential screening cost is not a turnkey Singapore project price. The current boundary does not fully price project-specific FOAK premium, financing structure, Singapore site/construction premium, licensing and security, detailed EPC/contingency, emergency-planning infrastructure, detailed 176.8 MWth IHX qualification, waste/decommissioning, insurance/liability, schedule risk, or negotiated cross-border CCS infrastructure/contracts. No monetary values are invented for these unresolved items. Their omission is why the economic conclusion is threshold compliance within the declared model, not bankability.

This distinction also strengthens the economics. The controlling S\$0/MWh case does not require electricity revenue. Instead, it charges the full selected source-product economic burden while giving no value to uncertain project power output. The doubled IHX/secondary-loop case remains only an adverse source cost sensitivity, not a capacity argument.

\subsection{Controlling deployment-stage economics}
\label{sec:stage-economics}
The preceding S\$3.725/tCO$_2$e calculation is retained as a \textbf{historical differential screening bridge}; it no longer controls the final economic conclusion. A stronger E1 accounting assigns the full normalized one-module nuclear burden without unsupported co-product sharing and gives approximately \textbf{S\$154/tCO$_2$e}, which fails the S\$100/t threshold.

E2B changes the physical utilisation architecture before applying an independently selected maturation method. INL/RPT-23-72972 supplies the medium non-electric advanced-reactor/HTGR method: a BOAK overnight-cost basis of USD6,000/kWe (2019 USD) and USD25/MWh OPEX is converted to a heat-only basis by removing the 9\% energy-conversion-system share and applying a 40\% HTGR electric-to-thermal conversion; the resulting BOAK heat-only bases are USD2,184/kWt and USD10/MWth-h~\cite{inl2023advancedcost}. FOAK applies the source medium premium of 1.6. Replication follows
\begin{equation}
C_N=C_1(1-LR)^{\log_2 N},
\end{equation}
where $C_N$ is overnight cost at the $N$th replicated unit, $C_1$ is the BOAK heat-only starting cost, $LR=0.10$ is the source medium learning rate and $N$ is cumulative unit number~\cite{inl2023advancedcost}. At $N=10$, this gives approximately USD1,539/kWt (2019 USD); OPEX is held at USD10/MWth-h as in the source example. These are modelled/projected maturation inputs, not observed HTGR construction costs, and they were selected independently of the CN4252 S\$100/t threshold.

The project then applies its documented 2019-to-2025 broad price escalation and SGD/USD conversion before annualising capital and adding O\&M. One 600 MWth source serves two real 176.8 MWth process trains, for 353.6 MWth useful reformer duty. No electricity revenue and no fictional heat-sharing customer are credited.

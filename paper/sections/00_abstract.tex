\begin{abstract}
Decarbonising natural-gas hydrogen production requires addressing both carbon released by reforming and emissions from firing the reformer furnace. This study evaluates a \textbf{nuclear-assisted SMR-H$_2$+CCS} concept in which high-temperature reactor heat replaces fired reformer heat while amine carbon capture manages process CO$_2$. The verified per-train chemical basis is INL Case 6: 130 MMSCFD H$_2$, 176.8 MWth reformer duty and high-temperature helium heat supply~\cite{inltev953,inltev961}. Architecture maturation combines two such trains with one 600 MWth GTHTR300C-class source, giving 353.6 MWth useful process heat, approximately 195,892 tH$_2$/y and approximately 1.834 MtCO$_2$e/y lifecycle abatement on the project E2B scaling basis. Stronger engineering-economic treatment overturns the historical low-cost screening bridge: the two-train FOAK case is approximately \textbf{S\$137.74/tCO$_2$e} and fails the cost threshold, while the early-commercial/BOAK and preferred mature 10-OAK cases are projected/modelled at approximately \textbf{S\$74.14/tCO$_2$e} and \textbf{S\$42.84/tCO$_2$e}, respectively. Conventional SMR+CCS remains the non-nuclear comparator; its matched-service E2 screen already exceeds the minimum abatement threshold, so nuclear is not claimed to be necessary merely to meet a minimum screening abatement threshold. Instead, nuclear process heat is the project's novel intervention and must justify its additional integration, siting, safety and regulatory complexity. Jurong Island is only a conditional industrial-integration context, and licensing-level integrated safety is not demonstrated. The preferred mature architecture is therefore a \textbf{conditional future feasibility case}, not present Singapore commercial or regulatory proof.
\end{abstract}

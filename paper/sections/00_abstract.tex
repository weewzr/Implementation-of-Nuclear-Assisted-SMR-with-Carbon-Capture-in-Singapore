\begin{abstract}
Singapore's CN4252 problem requires more than 0.25 MtCO$_2$e/y abatement at less than S\$100/tCO$_2$e. This study evaluates a literature-anchored nuclear-assisted steam-methane-reforming plant with CCS. The chemical-process basis is INL's 871$^\circ$C reformer, S/C=3.0, 925$^\circ$C HTGR / 900$^\circ$C process-heat Case 6, with 176.8 MWth process heat and 130 MMSCFD H$_2$~\cite{inltev953,inltev961}. The final hardware/economic screen maps that duty onto Nishihara et al.'s coherent 600 MWth GTHTR300C architecture, whose source configuration allocates 370 MWth to hydrogen heat and produces 88 MWe from the complementary 230 MWth power branch~\cite{nishihara2007potential}. At 85\% availability the process produces approximately 97,946 tH$_2$/y and the declared lifecycle boundary gives approximately 0.917 MtCO$_2$e/y avoided. A project-derived part-load mapping gives 161.92 MWe gross and 144.62 MWe net export; the zero-electricity-value economic screen is approximately S\$3.8/tCO$_2$e and the S\$150/MWh screen approximately $-$S\$172/tCO$_2$e. These are \textbf{conditional model results}, not observed Singapore commercial costs: the INL process and JAEA cogeneration architecture are separate source systems joined by an explicit project integration calculation, and cross-border CCS, lifecycle proxies and market-value assumptions remain conditions.
\end{abstract}

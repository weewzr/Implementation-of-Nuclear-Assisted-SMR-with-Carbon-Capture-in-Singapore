\begin{abstract}
This study tests nuclear-assisted steam methane reforming (SMR) with carbon capture and storage (CCS) as a Singapore low-carbon-hydrogen pathway against the CN4252 requirements of more than 0.25 MtCO2e/y abatement and less than S\$100/tCO2e. Rather than assuming the candidate succeeds, a source-anchored full-species screening model was constructed and challenged against material, thermodynamic, heat-integration, lifecycle and economic constraints. The verified model couples NG/steam-derived feed, ideal-gas SMR/WGS equilibrium, explicit CO2 removal, PSA recovery, inert purge, candidate-specific CCS duties, a finite-approach secondary-helium heat cascade, lifecycle emissions and forward abatement-cost accounting. A coupled 64-case uncertainty experiment produced zero cases satisfying both assignment thresholds; the conservative credible case fails both. Source-backed IEAGHG Case 1A conventional SMR+CCS exceeds the annual direct-abatement scale at the same H2 production rate, although its Singapore S\$/t result cannot be established on a common currency/year basis from available evidence. Within the tested evidence-backed domain, nuclear-assisted SMR+CCS is therefore not demonstrated to satisfy both CN4252 requirements or to be the preferred Singapore solution. Electrified SMR and electrolysis remain relevant comparators, but this project does not contain sufficiently matched Singapore lifecycle/economic evidence to rank them.
\end{abstract}

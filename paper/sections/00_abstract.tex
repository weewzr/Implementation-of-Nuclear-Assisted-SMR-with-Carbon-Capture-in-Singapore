\begin{abstract}
This study tests nuclear-assisted steam methane reforming (SMR) with carbon capture and storage (CCS) as a Singapore low-carbon-hydrogen pathway against the CN4252 requirements of more than 0.25 MtCO2e/y abatement and less than S\$100/tCO2e. Rather than assuming the candidate succeeds, a source-anchored full-species screening model was constructed and challenged against material, thermodynamic, heat-integration, lifecycle and economic constraints. The verified model couples NG/steam-derived feed, ideal-gas SMR/WGS equilibrium, explicit CO2 removal, PSA recovery, inert purge, candidate-specific CCS duties, a finite-approach secondary-helium heat cascade, lifecycle emissions and forward abatement-cost accounting. A coupled 64-case uncertainty experiment produced zero cases satisfying both assignment thresholds; the conservative credible case fails both. Source-backed IEAGHG Case 1A conventional SMR+CCS exceeds the annual direct-abatement scale at the same H2 production rate, although its Singapore S\$/t result cannot be established on a common currency/year basis from available evidence. Within the original tested evidence-backed domain, nuclear-assisted SMR+CCS is therefore not demonstrated to satisfy both CN4252 requirements. A separate post-canonical deployment-scale extension was subsequently remediated after Independent Review 5 using JAEA's directly supported helium-heated steam-reforming temperature state and one-module GTHTR300C cogeneration economics. At the source-supported 880$^\circ$C helium-inlet / approximately 600$^\circ$C process-outlet state, the process re-solve has non-positive lifecycle abatement, so no finite deployment scale can satisfy the annual-abatement threshold. The previously reported deployment conditional pass is therefore superseded. Electrified SMR and electrolysis remain relevant comparators, but this project does not contain sufficiently matched Singapore lifecycle/economic evidence to rank them.
\end{abstract}

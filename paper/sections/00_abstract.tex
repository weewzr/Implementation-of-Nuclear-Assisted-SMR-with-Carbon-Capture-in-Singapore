\begin{abstract}
Singapore's CN4252 problem requires more than 0.25 MtCO$_2$e/y of abatement at less than S\$100/tCO$_2$e. This study evaluates whether high-temperature nuclear heat can replace fired reformer heat in steam-methane reforming (SMR) while carbon capture and storage (CCS) manages process carbon. The chemical-process basis is INL's Case 6: an 871$^\circ$C reformer supplied with 900$^\circ$C process heat from a 925$^\circ$C HTGR outlet, requiring 176.8 MWth and producing 130 MMSCFD H$_2$~\cite{inltev953,inltev961}. An application-specific reactor screen then retains a 600 MWth GTHTR300C-class architecture because it provides the required high-temperature secondary-helium/process-heat configuration; representative water-cooled SMRs require temperature augmentation, HTTR is too small, and HTR-PM is used primarily as operating HTGR safety/licensing evidence.  The approximately 370 MWth JAEA value is a source process-heat branch class, not one physical exchanger~\cite{nishihara2007potential}. At 85\% availability, hydrogen production is approximately 97,946 t/y. The declared lifecycle boundary gives approximately 0.917 MtCO$_2$e/y avoided and a candidate intensity of 1.95 kgCO$_2$e/kgH$_2$. Because no independently validated off-design project power output is available, the controlling economic case credits \textbf{S\$0/MWh} of electricity revenue while retaining the full selected reactor economic burden; the resulting abatement cost is approximately \textbf{S\$3.725/tCO$_2$e}. Both CN4252 numerical thresholds are therefore satisfied under the declared literature-anchored screening assumptions. This is a \textbf{conditional model result}, not demonstrated Singapore commercial feasibility; detailed reactor/process integration, lifecycle proxies and cross-border CO$_2$ storage remain material limitations.
\end{abstract}

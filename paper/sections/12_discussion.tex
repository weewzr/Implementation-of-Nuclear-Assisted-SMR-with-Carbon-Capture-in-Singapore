\section{Discussion}
The final result is controlled by five linked features. First, the INL 871$^\circ$C reformer basis preserves industrially relevant methane conversion while the 900$^\circ$C nuclear heat-delivery case removes the need for a fired trim reformer~\cite{inltev953,inltev961}. Second, the common INL baseline makes the direct carbon benefit unusually transparent: nuclear heat reduces fuel use while CCS removes most process carbon. Third, one 600 MWth reactor has substantial output beyond the 176.8 MWth hydrogen heat service, so cogeneration closure is economically material. Fourth, the final duty slightly exceeds JAEA's 170 MWth reference IHX; using the doubled-IHX cost sensitivity avoids understating that integration burden. Fifth, CCS transport/storage and Singapore nuclear deployment remain external infrastructure conditions.

The result is therefore conditional rather than predictive. Mature GTHTR300C economics are design-study values, not observed Singapore project bids. Electricity value can materially change net cost, but the zero-value case is retained to prevent the pass/fail result from depending on an assumed lucrative offtake. Modern-central and FOAK/adverse reactor-cost cases test whether the conclusion survives less favourable capital assumptions.

The project history remains scientifically useful but is no longer the headline result. The initial 64-case screen and Review-5 600$^\circ$C mock-up state exposed important accounting and heat-interface failure modes; they are preserved in the repository. The final design instead follows the strongest directly relevant primary process literature.

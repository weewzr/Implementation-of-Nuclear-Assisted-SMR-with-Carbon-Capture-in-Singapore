\section{Final literature-supported design and method}
\label{sec:final-design}

\subsection{Design selection}
The final chemical-process basis is INL/NGNP Case 6. INL selected an 871$^\circ$C reformer outlet, steam/carbon ratio 3.0 and 88\% PSA recovery~\cite{inltev953}; TEV-961 reports that a 925$^\circ$C HTGR outlet supplies 900$^\circ$C process heat and the full 176.8 MWth reforming duty at that target~\cite{inltev961}. The source process produces 130 MMSCFD H$_2$ from 34.0 MMSCFD natural gas with 17.3 MWe process demand, 1,927 short ton/day captured CO$_2$ and 142 short ton/day emitted CO$_2$~\cite{inltev961}. The same INL model family provides the 130-MMSCFD conventional no-capture baseline: 52.5 MMSCFD natural gas and 3,205 short ton/day emitted CO$_2$~\cite{inltev953}.

\subsection{Deriving the reactor requirement from the chemical process}
The reactor is not selected first.  The verified chemical-process requirement is the starting point.  INL Case 6 requires 176.8 MWth and reports an 871$^\circ$C reformer outlet.  The same source supplies secondary helium at 900$^\circ$C, giving the positive reported-location temperature difference
\[
\Delta T_{\rm process,reported}=900-871=29~^\circ\mathrm{C}.
\]
This is not claimed as the minimum local reformer pinch: local tube-wall, catalyst-bed and gas-temperature profiles are not all available from the source.

INL then places the secondary-helium supply 25$^\circ$C below its selected reactor-outlet case:
\[
T_{\rm reactor,out}=T_{\rm He,secondary}+\Delta T_{\rm IHX}
=900+25=925~^\circ\mathrm{C}.
\]
Thus the evidence chain is
\[
925~^\circ\mathrm{C}\rightarrow900~^\circ\mathrm{C}
\rightarrow871~^\circ\mathrm{C},
\]
with 176.8 MWth transferred at the process interface~\cite{inltev961}.  The secondary-helium energy balance is independently consistent:
\[
\bar c_p=\frac{176.8\times10^3}
{78.49(900-466)}
\approx5.19~\mathrm{kJ\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}.
\]

\subsection{Technology screen: why an HTGR and why GTHTR300C class?}
Representative water-cooled SMRs provide useful electricity, steam, desalination and lower-temperature process heat, but their water-cooled outlet temperatures are far below the approximately 900$^\circ$C heating-medium requirement.  They therefore require a separate temperature-augmentation route for this direct-heat application.  This is an application-specific temperature mismatch, not a claim that LWRs are generally inferior.

HTTR passes the temperature filter: its 30 MWth reactor has demonstrated 950$^\circ$C operation~\cite{jaeahttr2026ops}.  It fails the project-scale filter because
\[
176.8/30=5.89,
\]
so HTTR is used as experimental evidence rather than as the project heat source.  HTR-PM provides valuable commercial HTGR operating, licensing and safety precedent, but its demonstrated plant is a steam-cycle architecture rather than the 900$^\circ$C secondary-helium reformer interface required here.  JAEA's GTHTR300C family, in contrast, is a 600 MWth process-heat/cogeneration design with 950$^\circ$C reactor-outlet and approximately 900$^\circ$C secondary-helium conditions~\cite{nishihara2007potential}.  It therefore passes the temperature, scale and process-heat-architecture filters.

The project is consequently described as a \textbf{GTHTR300C-class / GTHTR300C-informed screening architecture}, not as an exact GTHTR300C plant: the project uses the INL 925/900/871$^\circ$C operating point and 176.8 MWth duty rather than the exact JAEA heat split.

\subsection{Thermal integration with the JAEA architecture}
INL supplies the chemical-process duty; Nishihara et al. supplies the selected GTHTR300C hardware/economic architecture. They are deliberately kept as separate evidence layers.

The selected source architecture is a 600 MWth GTHTR300C-class design.  JAEA's high-hydrogen variant provides approximately 371 MWth of process-heat branch capability (rounded to 370 MWth in the canonical economic model) and approximately 87--88 MWe of complementary source electricity~\cite{nishihara2007potential}.  The 370 MWth value is therefore a \emph{source process-heat branch/economic architecture}, not the rating of one physical IHX. The INL process requires 176.8 MWth, so nominal source-branch utilisation is
\[
176.8/370=47.8\%,
\]
with 193.2 MWth nominal branch margin.  This does not prove exchanger feasibility.  The published GTHTR300C reference IHX is approximately 170 MWth, so
\[
176.8-170=6.8~\mathrm{MW_{th}},\qquad 176.8/170=1.040.
\]
The project duty is therefore about 4.0\% above one published conceptual reference-IHX duty; a larger, parallel or otherwise redesigned exchanger arrangement remains a qualification requirement. At reactor-rating level,
\[
600=176.8+423.2\ {\rm MW_{th}}.
\]
The 423.2 MWth term is reported only as \emph{remaining thermal capacity}. It is not converted into a project electricity-output claim because the available source does not provide a validated off-design power-cycle/internal-load model for this changed heat split.

For the process-side helium screen, INL Case 6 provides approximately 900$^\circ$C supply, 466$^\circ$C return and 78.49 kg/s. Using an average $c_p=5.2$ kJ kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$ gives
\[
\dot m_{He}=\frac{176.8\times10^3}{5.2(900-466)}\approx78.34\ {\rm kg/s},
\]
which is used only as a constant-$c_p$ verification screen~\cite{inltev961}.

\subsection{Lifecycle calculation}
Source direct emissions are annualized at the selected mature-design 85\% availability. Lifecycle accounting adds the difference in upstream natural-gas burden and subtracts nuclear-heat, incremental auxiliary-electricity and CO$_2$ transport/storage burdens. The upstream-gas factor is a global proxy~\cite{iea2026lng}; the nuclear lifecycle proxy is global rather than Singapore-specific~\cite{unece2022lca}. The CCS transport burden remains an explicit screening assumption.

\subsection{Forward economics}
The principal mature case uses the coherent Nishihara reference economics: 59.7 billion JPY plant cost, 0.52 JPY/MJ heat and 4.9 JPY/kWh electricity at 85\% availability~\cite{nishihara2007potential}. Because no validated project off-design electricity output is claimed, the controlling economic test assigns \textbf{S\$0/MWh electricity export value}. To avoid free reactor capacity, the calculation charges the full published source-product burden: the source 370 MWth heat product at its source heat cost plus the source 88 MWe generation at its source electricity cost. This is a conservative economic allocation, not a claim that the project itself exports 88 MWe.

The source's 70.9 billion JPY / 0.57 JPY/MJ / 5.5 JPY/kWh case is retained only as an adverse \emph{cost} sensitivity in which assigned IHX/secondary-loop cost doubles; it is not interpreted as increased exchanger capacity. CCS capital is anchored to IEAGHG Case 1A and T\&S remains a Singapore screening scenario~\cite{ieaghg2017smr,mticcs2025cost}.

Forward abatement cost remains
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm abatement}=\frac{C_{\rm candidate}-C_{\rm baseline}}{A_{\rm avoided}}.
\end{equation}

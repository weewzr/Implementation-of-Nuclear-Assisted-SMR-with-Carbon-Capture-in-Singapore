\section{Final literature-supported design and method}
\label{sec:final-design}

\subsection{Design selection}
The final chemical-process basis is INL/NGNP Case 6. INL selected an 871$^\circ$C reformer outlet, steam/carbon ratio 3.0 and 88\% PSA recovery~\cite{inltev953}; TEV-961 reports that a 925$^\circ$C HTGR outlet supplies 900$^\circ$C process heat and the full 176.8 MWth reforming duty at that target~\cite{inltev961}. The source process produces 130 MMSCFD H$_2$ from 34.0 MMSCFD natural gas with 17.3 MWe process demand, 1,927 short ton/day captured CO$_2$ and 142 short ton/day emitted CO$_2$~\cite{inltev961}. The same INL model family provides the 130-MMSCFD conventional no-capture baseline: 52.5 MMSCFD natural gas and 3,205 short ton/day emitted CO$_2$~\cite{inltev953}.

\subsection{Mapping the INL process onto the JAEA cogeneration architecture}
INL supplies the chemical-process duty; JAEA supplies a candidate reactor/IHX/cogeneration architecture. They are not treated as the same source flowsheet.

The mature economic architecture selected from Nishihara et al. is a 600 MWth GTHTR300C with a 370 MWth source hydrogen/IHX branch and 88 MWe gross electricity from the complementary 230 MWth power branch~\cite{nishihara2007potential}. The final INL process requires only 176.8 MWth, below that source heat-branch capacity. The project therefore retains the 600 MWth reactor and maps the residual
\[
Q_{\rm power}=600-176.8=423.2\ {\rm MW_{th}}
\]
through the source-implied gross conversion
\[
\eta_{\rm GT}=\frac{88}{600-370}=0.3826,
\]
giving a \textbf{PROJECT-DERIVED} gross electricity screen of approximately 161.92 MWe. After the source process demand of 17.3 MWe, the corresponding net export screen is 144.62 MWe. This is an engineering mapping for the project, not a published JAEA part-load operating point.

For the process-side helium screen, the project now uses INL Case-6's approximately 900$^\circ$C supply and 466$^\circ$C return. With an average $c_p=5.2$ kJ kg$^{-1}$ K$^{-1}$,
\[
\dot m_{He}=\frac{176.8\times10^3}{5.2(900-466)}\approx78.34\ {\rm kg/s},
\]
close to INL's reported 78.49 kg/s~\cite{inltev961}. The constant-$c_p$ value is a screening approximation.

\subsection{Lifecycle calculation}
Source direct emissions are annualized at the selected mature-design 85\% availability. Lifecycle accounting adds the difference in upstream natural-gas burden and subtracts nuclear-heat, incremental auxiliary-electricity and CO$_2$ transport/storage burdens. The upstream-gas factor is a global proxy~\cite{iea2026lng}; the nuclear lifecycle proxy is global rather than Singapore-specific~\cite{unece2022lca}. The CCS transport burden remains an explicit screening assumption.

\subsection{Forward economics}
Historical JAEA and IEAGHG anchors use the Review-5 common cost-year/FX normalization. The principal mature case uses the coherent Nishihara reference economics: 59.7 billion JPY plant cost, 0.52 JPY/MJ heat and 4.9 JPY/kWh electricity~\cite{nishihara2007potential}. The source's 70.9 billion JPY / 0.57 JPY/MJ / 5.5 JPY/kWh case is retained only as an adverse \emph{cost} sensitivity in which the assigned IHX/secondary-loop cost is doubled; it is not interpreted as doubled exchanger capacity.

The full source-priced heat and project-derived power burden is charged. Only net electricity after the 17.3 MWe process load receives a scenario value. S$0/MWh is the conservative no-value test; EMA's market context motivates S$100--200/MWh sensitivity cases~\cite{emausep2025}. CCS capital is anchored to IEAGHG Case 1A and T\&S remains a Singapore screening scenario~\cite{ieaghg2017smr,mticcs2025cost}.

Forward abatement cost remains
\begin{equation}
C_{\rm abatement}=\frac{C_{\rm candidate}-C_{\rm baseline}}{A_{\rm avoided}}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Historical research boundary}
The earlier fixed-scale 64-case study and the Review-5 deployment study remain reproducible audit evidence. They are not the operative final-design calculation.

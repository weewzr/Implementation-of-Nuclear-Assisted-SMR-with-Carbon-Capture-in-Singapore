\section{Design basis and system boundary}\label{sec:design-basis}
W3 defined the physical configuration. This section fixes the numbers and accounting boundaries that make that configuration calculable. The project deliberately layers evidence rather than pretending that one source describes an already-built plant: Idaho National Laboratory (INL) supplies the chemical/process operating basis; Japan Atomic Energy Agency/Japan Atomic Energy Research Institute (JAEA/JAERI) and Nishihara supply the high-temperature reactor/process-heat design architecture and source economics; HTTR supplies demonstrated high-temperature precedent; and the project combines those layers through declared assumptions, engineering balances and Rust calculations. Table~\ref{tab:principal-design-basis} makes those distinctions explicit.

\subsection{Preferred project architecture and per-train provenance}
The final proposed CN4252 solution is \textbf{nuclear-assisted SMR-H$_2$+CCS}. The INL quantities below are retained as a \emph{per-train source-model basis}. E2B constructs the preferred future architecture by duplicating two identical 130-MMSCFD chemical trains while retaining one 600 MWth GTHTR300C-class source. Thus the project-derived preferred totals are 260 MMSCFD H$_2$, 353.6 MWth reformer heat, 68 MMSCFD candidate natural gas and approximately 1.085 MtCO$_2$/y captured. At the adopted 85\% annualisation basis this corresponds to approximately 195,892 tH$_2$/y. The two-train plant is a project-derived screening architecture; it is not claimed to be a published JAEA or INL plant.

The approximately 370/371 MWth JAEA process-heat branch is an architecture-level source value, not one physical IHX~\cite{nishihara2007potential}. Two 176.8 MWth trains require 353.6 MWth, which is below that branch. Component qualification, coupled dynamics and actual manifold/IHX configuration remain deployment gates.

\subsection{Common hydrogen service and conventional baseline}
The comparison holds hydrogen service fixed so that emissions or cost do not improve merely because one case produces less product. INL reports 130 million standard cubic feet per day (MMSCFD), approximately 29,000 lb/h, of H$_2$ for the common service~\cite{inltev953,inltev961}. The project annualises the source mass rate at the 85\% design-study availability basis to approximately 97,946 t H$_2$/y; that annual value is a \textbf{project-derived result}, not an INL-reported annual production.

The controlling reference is INL's conventional, fired, no-capture SMR-H$_2$ case at the same hydrogen service. Its aggregate natural-gas use is 52.5 MMSCFD and its direct emitted CO$_2$ is 3,205 short ton/day~\cite{inltev953}. The fired reformer supplies the high-temperature duty described in W3. The reduced lifecycle model also retains 6.3 MWe as a \textbf{project screening assumption/historical common-electricity anchor}; the repository does not establish that value as an INL Case-6/common-output measurement. This distinction matters later because only the candidate-minus-baseline electricity increment is charged in the lifecycle screen.

\subsection{Candidate chemical-process basis}
The candidate chemical plant is INL Case 6. These are source states/model results, not quantities re-derived by this project. The candidate uses 34.0 MMSCFD natural gas and produces the same 130 MMSCFD (approximately 29,000 lb/h) H$_2$ service~\cite{inltev953,inltev961}. Its reformer outlet is 871$^\circ$C at a source process pressure of 31.7 bar, with steam/carbon ratio 3.0 mol/mol, reported methane conversion 78.1\%, and PSA H$_2$ recovery 88\%~\cite{inltev953,inltev961}. The 88\% quantity is recovery, not hydrogen purity.

The same source case requires 176.8 MWth of process heat and 17.3 MWe of chemical-process electricity~\cite{inltev961}. It reports 1,927 short ton/day of captured CO$_2$ and 142 short ton/day of residual emitted CO$_2$~\cite{inltev961}. For heat transfer, the source reactor-outlet case is 925$^\circ$C and the process-side secondary helium is 900$^\circ$C supply, approximately 466$^\circ$C return and 78.49 kg/s~\cite{inltev961}. These values define the temperature and duty that the selected reactor architecture must serve; W5 tests their thermal compatibility rather than replacing them with invented local wall/pinch states.

\subsection{GTHTR300C-class reactor design basis}
The reactor basis is a \textbf{GTHTR300C-class / GTHTR300C-informed screening architecture}, not an exact operating GTHTR300C plant. JAEA/JAERI/Nishihara literature supplies a 600 MWth high-temperature helium reactor design class, approximately 950$^\circ$C reactor-outlet precedent and approximately 900$^\circ$C secondary-helium process-heat architecture~\cite{jaeri2004gthtr300c,nishihara2007potential}. The high-H$_2$ source variant contains an approximately 370/371 MWth process-heat branch and an approximately 87--88 MWe complementary electricity product~\cite{nishihara2007potential}. The latter is retained only in the published source economic allocation; it is \textbf{not} a claimed project gross, net or export output.

Published GTHTR300C evidence also provides an approximately 170 MWth reference physical IHX duty. That component reference is distinct from the 370/371 MWth architecture-level process-heat branch and does not establish qualification of a 176.8 MWth project exchanger~\cite{jaeri2004gthtr300c,nishihara2007potential}. Combining the 600 MWth source reactor rating with the INL 176.8 MWth project duty leaves 423.2 MWth by subtraction. This is a \textbf{project-derived remaining thermal capacity identity}; it is not electricity, turbine input, cooling duty or waste heat.

\subsection{Annualisation and principal screening assumptions}
The 85\% availability basis corresponds to 7,446 operating hours per year and annualises the instantaneous source rates~\cite{nishihara2007potential}. It is a design-study assumption, not evidence that a future Singapore reactor/process complex will achieve 85\% availability.

Only assumptions that materially control later lifecycle/economic results are surfaced here. Natural-gas energy conversion uses a \textbf{project screening assumption} of 1,044 Btu/scf. The lifecycle screen uses an IEA global upstream-gas proxy of 11.5 kgCO$_2$e/GJ~\cite{iea2026lng}, a UNECE nuclear-electricity lifecycle anchor of 5.5 kgCO$_2$e/MWh$_e$~\cite{unece2022lca}, a \textbf{project allocation proxy} of 0.504 MWh$_e$-equivalent/MWh$_{th}$ for direct heat, and a \textbf{project screening assumption} of 0.025 tCO$_2$e per tonne of CO$_2$ transported/stored. These proxies are used to make the lifecycle boundary explicit; none is presented as a Singapore route-specific measurement or a project-specific HTGR heat LCA.

For economics, the natural-gas price is a \textbf{project screening assumption} of S\$15/GJ. Nishihara source economics supply 0.52 JPY/MJ heat, 4.9 JPY/kWh electricity and 59.7 billion JPY plant cost before the project's documented price-year/currency normalization~\cite{nishihara2007potential}. CCS capital uses the IEAGHG reference basis and 8\%/25-y annualisation convention~\cite{ieaghg2017smr}. The model adds a \textbf{project screening} 10\% integration/site capital allowance, annualised at the retained 3\%/40-y design-study convention, plus a \textbf{project screening} S\$15/tCO$_2$ transport/storage tariff. These are screening inputs, not vendor quotations or contracted Singapore tariffs.

\subsection{Lifecycle, economic and deployment boundaries}
The \textbf{chemical-process boundary} contains natural-gas/steam feed, reforming, WGS, CO$_2$ capture, PSA and H$_2$ product. The \textbf{nuclear process-heat boundary} contains reactor heat, primary helium, the IHX, secondary helium and delivery of process heat. Direct emissions use the source-reported baseline and candidate CO$_2$ streams rather than reverse-engineering them from an assumed pure-methane feed.

The \textbf{lifecycle extension} adds upstream natural-gas emissions, the declared nuclear lifecycle proxy, incremental auxiliary electricity and CO$_2$ transport/storage burden. It does not claim a Singapore-specific gas supply-chain inventory, project-specific nuclear process-heat LCA, dynamic capture/outage model, or complete construction/decommissioning inventory.

The \textbf{economic boundary} represents baseline and candidate natural gas, the full selected source heat/electricity economic burden, CCS capital annualisation, the integration/site screening allowance and CO$_2$ transport/storage. Project electricity revenue is fixed at \textbf{S\$0/y}. No validated off-design turbine/internal-load model exists for the changed 176.8 MWth heat split, so the paper claims no exact project gross MWe, net MWe or export MWe. The source 87--88 MWe product remains only part of the published source architecture/economic burden.

The \textbf{deployment boundary} remains outside demonstrated model closure: no Singapore nuclear site or licence is assumed; detailed project nuclear/chemical safety and emergency planning are not solved here; no operating cross-border CCS route/storage contract is assumed; and the economic screen is not bankable First-of-a-Kind (FOAK) project economics.

\subsection{Values deliberately not invented}
The source set does not support a complete set of detailed stream/equipment states. WGS inlet/outlet temperatures, detailed PSA feed/tail-gas state points, CO$_2$ compressor discharge pressure, project helium pressure drop, exchanger wall temperatures, a local reformer pinch and an exact project electricity output are therefore \textbf{not assigned}. Their absence is a model limitation, not permission to fill the table with fabricated precision.

The resulting design basis is intentionally mixed but traceable: source process states define the chemical service, source reactor studies define a compatible architecture, project assumptions close specified screening boundaries, and project calculations produce the annual/lifecycle/economic outputs. Given those states and boundaries, the next question is how the governing equations test thermal compatibility and propagate the inputs through the model; that is the purpose of the model-formulation and heat-integration sections that follow.

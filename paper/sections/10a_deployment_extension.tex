\section{Singapore deployment-scale techno-economic extension}
\label{sec:deployment-extension}
The original Gate-5 result remains 64 fixed-scale cases with 0 joint passes. Independent Review~5 invalidated the earlier deployment conditional pass because its heat interface and one-module economic allocation were not source-equivalent. This section reports the corrected deployment extension.

\subsection{Source-supported heat interface and process re-solve}
JAEA's HTTR steam-reforming specifications distinguish the temperatures explicitly: secondary helium enters the steam reformer at 880$^\circ$C; process gas enters at 450$^\circ$C and leaves at approximately 580--600$^\circ$C; helium leaves at approximately 585--650$^\circ$C~\cite{jaea2018excellent,jaea2007mockup}. The corrected deployment therefore uses 950$^\circ$C reactor primary-helium outlet, 900$^\circ$C secondary helium at the GTHTR300C IHX outlet, 880$^\circ$C helium at the reformer inlet, and 600$^\circ$C reacting-gas outlet. The old 900/920$^\circ$C deployment chemistry is not retained.

The automated heat-interface acceptance checks require positive primary-to-secondary and helium-to-process driving forces. The source-anchored hot-end differences are 50 K and 280 K. Recycle, reforming equilibrium, WGS, PSA, capture throughput, heat duty, helium flow and lifecycle emissions are re-solved at this lower process state.

The corrected result is adverse: the source-defined process state has non-positive lifecycle abatement relative to the common baseline. Therefore no finite multiplication of this state can satisfy the absolute 0.25 MtCO$_2$e/y criterion. Scaling a non-positive specific abatement cannot create positive avoided emissions.

\subsection{One-module GTHTR300C economic closure}
The mature economic reconstruction now uses the primary GTHTR300C cogeneration study rather than transferring the later four-unit 0.7 JPY/MJ shortcut. The source reports 59.7 billion JPY total nuclear-plant cost including 11.2 billion JPY for the IHX and secondary-helium loop, 0.52 JPY/MJ heat, 4.9 JPY/kWh electricity and 85\% availability. Doubling the IHX/secondary-loop cost raises total plant cost to 70.9 billion JPY, heat to 0.57 JPY/MJ and electricity to 5.5 JPY/kWh~\cite{nishihara2007potential}. The source architecture produces about 202 MWe with 170 MWth process heat~\cite{jaeri2004gthtr300c}.

The corrected model charges the full reactor economic burden as source-priced heat plus source-priced electricity, then treats electricity value as a separate credit. No reactor capacity disappears through allocation. EMA reports that 2025 USEP largely settled in the S\$100--200/MWh range~\cite{emausep2025}; zero, S\$100, S\$150 and S\$200/MWh cases are evaluated explicitly. Since lifecycle abatement is non-positive, no finite electricity value can create a CN4252 joint pass.

FX is then applied using explicitly dated 29 September 2026 screening rates. This is a transparent screening normalization, not a nuclear-specific construction index.

\subsection{Review-5 conclusion}
The corrected conditional pass does \textbf{not} survive. The earlier S\$47.637/t arithmetic was reproducible for its assumptions, but those assumptions used a non-source-equivalent heat interface and incomplete one-module cogeneration closure. Under the directly supported JAEA helium-heated-SMR temperature state, the process re-solve does not produce positive lifecycle abatement on the common boundary. The controlling barrier after remediation is therefore physical/lifecycle performance at the source-supported reforming state, not a tunable electricity price.

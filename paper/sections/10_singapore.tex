\section{Singapore implementation implications}
The corrected model translates the candidate into annual H2, fresh-NG energy, captured/residual CO2, cross-border storage throughput, nuclear process heat and helium-loop scale. Required thermal service is expressed against the source-backed 170 MW JAEA IHX benchmark rather than converted into a fabricated commercial reactor-module count.

Singapore deployment remains conditional~\cite{mti2025nuclear,mti2026inir,mti2025carbon,mti2025indonesiaccs}. Nuclear power has not been assumed to be deployed: current official activity is capability-building and infrastructure-readiness assessment. Cross-border CO2 transport/storage is likewise treated as an emerging regional pathway under bilateral cooperation, not an existing domestic geological storage service. An implementation roadmap would therefore require, at minimum, nuclear regulatory/readiness development, site and safety assessment, a demonstrated process-heat coupling architecture, and contracted cross-border CO2 transport/storage.


For historical traceability, the \emph{original fixed-scale Gate-5/R3 screening quantities} are reported in Table~\ref{tab:singapore-scale}; they are not the Review-5-remediated deployment state in Section~\ref{sec:deployment-extension}. They are generated from the corrected R3 state and deliberately retain claim-strength labels. In particular, the \(170\) MW IHX ratio is a duty-equivalence indicator against the GTHTR300C engineering benchmark~\cite{jaeri2004gthtr300c}; it is not a count of commercial reactor modules.

The storage throughput satisfies the model identity
\begin{equation}
\dot m_{\mathrm{CO_2,storage}}=
\dot m_{\mathrm{CO_2,process\ capture}}+
\dot m_{\mathrm{CO_2,purge\ capture}},
\end{equation}
which is regression-tested in Rust. This quantity defines the cross-border CCS service that would have to be available to the candidate; it does not establish that such capacity has been contracted or permitted in Singapore.

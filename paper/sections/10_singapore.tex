\subsection{Singapore feasibility and implementation roadmap}

\subsubsection{Scale and infrastructure}
The preferred future two-train architecture produces approximately 195,892 tH$_2$/y and captures approximately 1.085 MtCO$_2$/y. One 600 MWth-class reactor module supplies two parallel 130 MMSCFD process trains requiring 353.6 MWth total reformer heat. These totals are project-derived duplication/scaling of the verified per-train INL basis, not source-reported operation of an integrated plant. This is industrial-scale infrastructure rather than a laboratory demonstration: it requires a nuclear site, an integrated hydrogen/CCS complex, natural-gas and water/steam services, CO$_2$ conditioning/export facilities and a dependable storage chain.

\subsubsection{Nuclear readiness in Singapore}
Singapore has \textbf{not made a decision to deploy nuclear energy}. The Government is building capability to assess advanced nuclear technologies, and an International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) Integrated Nuclear Infrastructure Review (INIR) Phase-1 mission is planned from 2027~\cite{mti2026inir}. Singapore's Ministry of Trade and Industry (MTI) states that any deployment decision must consider safety, reliability, affordability and environmental sustainability, while the Energy Market Authority (EMA) describes safety as an overriding priority for a small, densely populated city-state~\cite{mti2026safetycriteria,ema2026safety}. The IAEA Milestones/INIR framework spans institutional issues including nuclear safety, radioactive waste and emergency planning. Consequently, regulatory framework, siting, emergency planning, safeguards/security, waste strategy, workforce and public/institutional readiness are deployment gates, not details solved by the process model.

\subsubsection{Siting and cooling: options, not a selected site}
No Singapore nuclear site is selected in this study.  Jurong Island is an obvious industrial-integration screening case, but its refinery/chemical concentration also creates external fire, explosion, toxic-release, security and emergency-planning questions.  The peer-reviewed Jurong modular-nuclear siting study is explicitly a preliminary optimisation/screening method using a virtual J-Park environment, not licensing or EPZ evidence~\cite{devanand2019siting}.

Other options remain open: another coastal/industrial site, underground siting, offshore/floating concepts, and future land.  Singapore's 2026 western-island plan explicitly allows for new power-generation infrastructure, but does not designate nuclear technology or a nuclear site~\cite{govsg2026western}.  Site selection therefore remains a future nuclear-safety/regulatory study rather than an optimisation performed here.

Cooling has the same two-sided character.  PUB confirms that seafront/Jurong Island industry can use once-through seawater cooling or seawater cooling towers, reducing freshwater dependence~\cite{pubseawater2026}.  Coastal access is therefore attractive for heat rejection, but a nuclear site must simultaneously address coastal flooding/sea-level/storm-surge hazards, intake blockage/fouling, corrosion, thermal discharge and marine impacts.  The project does not calculate seawater flow because the exact project heat-rejection duty is not yet defined: the 423.2 MWth remaining reactor capacity is \emph{not} automatically waste heat.

\subsubsection{CCS feasibility}
Singapore does not have suitable domestic geological CO$_2$ storage resources and is pursuing cross-border CCS with regional partners~\cite{mti2026carbon}. Cooperation with Indonesia and Malaysia has advanced through government agreements / memoranda of understanding (MOUs), but an agreement is not an operating transport-and-storage service. MTI continues to study the complete CCS value chain and its costs~\cite{mticcs2025cost,mti2025indonesiaccs}. The approximately 542 ktCO$_2$/y captured by this design therefore requires contracted conditioning, transport, injection, monitoring and liability arrangements outside Singapore. CCS availability is a binding deployment dependency for the proposed CCS architecture even though the assignment's numerical abatement threshold is surprisingly robust to partial capture.  The Rust screening sensitivity finds approximately 0.388 MtCO$_2$e/y avoided even if none of the canonical captured stream is stored, because nuclear heat still displaces furnace gas and upstream gas burden.  That endpoint has much higher direct emissions and is not a capture-plant outage model; it therefore does not justify deleting CCS from the proposed deep-decarbonisation configuration.

\subsubsection{Natural-gas transition character}
The candidate still consumes 34 MMSCFD natural gas. Nuclear heat removes fired reformer duty but does not remove methane as feedstock. Upstream gas emissions remain inside the lifecycle boundary, which is why this study describes the pathway as lower-carbon hydrogen rather than zero-carbon hydrogen. Continued imported-gas exposure and methane-supply-chain performance remain transition risks.

\subsubsection{Economic feasibility versus bankability}
The verified approximately S\$3.725/tCO$_2$e is a \emph{modelled screening abatement cost}, not a quotation for a Singapore nuclear project. It uses mature design-study reactor economics, explicit normalization, a screening CCS cost structure and zero project electricity revenue. It does not constitute a bankable estimate of First-of-a-Kind (FOAK) financing, Singapore site premium, licensing, security, detailed EPC, contingency, decommissioning/waste, insurance, schedule risk or negotiated CCS contracts. The low numerical cost therefore establishes threshold compliance within the declared model boundary, not commercial investment readiness.

\subsubsection{Staged implementation roadmap}
Because several prerequisites are institutional or infrastructure-dependent, implementation is better expressed as decision gates than unsupported dates:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Evidence and capability development:} maintain primary-source HTGR/process-heat evidence, fuel/material qualification awareness and independent model verification. Gate: no unresolved scientific blocker to the screening concept.
\item \textbf{Singapore nuclear readiness:} complete national technology, safety, regulatory, siting, emergency-planning, waste/security and institutional studies, including the INIR process. Gate: Government determines that nuclear deployment can proceed to project development.
\item \textbf{CCS chain qualification:} establish legally/commercially usable cross-border transport and permanent-storage arrangements with monitoring and liability terms. Gate: bankable storage service for the required CO$_2$ throughput.
\item \textbf{Vendor/component qualification:} select a reactor/process-heat architecture and qualify the high-temperature IHX, secondary helium loop, materials and process isolation concept. Gate: component design closes temperature, pressure, creep/fatigue and inspection requirements.
\item \textbf{Non-nuclear integration demonstration:} test control logic, reformer heat-demand transients, isolation/trips and process-safe-state behaviour using representative heat-loop hardware where practicable. Gate: integrated transient/control hazards are acceptably bounded.
\item \textbf{Nuclear safety and licensing case:} perform site-specific nuclear safety analysis plus chemical-to-nuclear hazard assessment, separation/layout analysis and emergency planning. Gate: regulatory approval pathway is credible.
\item \textbf{FOAK project decision:} complete Front-End Engineering Design (FEED) / Engineering, Procurement and Construction (EPC) cost, financing, contracts, schedule, security, waste/decommissioning and offtake assessment. Gate: investment decision.
\item \textbf{Construction, commissioning and operation:} proceed only after preceding gates close; verify performance and emissions rather than assuming screening values.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Cooling-screen notation}
E3 uses the sensible-heat relation
\[
\dot m_w=\frac{\dot Q}{c_{p,w}\Delta T_w},
\]
where $\dot m_w$ is seawater mass flow (kg/s), $\dot Q$ is the screened heat load (kW), $c_{p,w}$ is seawater specific heat (approximately 4.0 kJ/kg-K for the screening calculation) and $\Delta T_w$ is the assumed temperature rise (K). Volumetric flow follows from $\dot V_w=\dot m_w/\rho_w$ using the declared seawater-density basis. For an illustrative 8 K rise, the 246.4 MWth full-power residual-disposition envelope gives approximately 7.53 m$^3$/s; a deliberately conservative full-600-MWth envelope gives approximately 18.34 m$^3$/s. These are \textbf{illustrative heat-sink envelopes}, not a designed normal cooling-water requirement and not reactor decay-heat/UHS duty. The actual cooling design requires the complete plant heat balance, decay-heat transient and candidate-site intake/outfall or tower design.

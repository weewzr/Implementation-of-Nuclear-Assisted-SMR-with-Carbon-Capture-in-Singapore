\section{Singapore implementation implications}
The final design requires one 600 MWth-class HTGR module coupled through an enlarged high-temperature IHX/secondary-helium train to an industrial SMR+CCS plant. The source process heat demand (176.8 MWth) uses less than one-third of reactor thermal rating; the remaining reactor output must therefore have a useful electricity or heat role rather than being treated as economically free. The forward model closes this through the GTHTR300C cogeneration architecture and explicit electricity-value sensitivities.

Implementation remains conditional. Singapore has no operating nuclear plant, so licensing, siting, emergency planning, safeguards, workforce development and high-temperature process-heat qualification remain prerequisites. Cross-border CO$_2$ transport and storage are likewise not assumed available at an authoritative tariff; MTI continues to treat CCS cost and infrastructure as developing evidence~\cite{mticcs2025cost}. The final model therefore treats nuclear deployment and T\&S as scenario conditions rather than existing infrastructure.

A staged implementation pathway is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item validate the 900$^\circ$C-class secondary-helium/IHX/reformer interface and safety isolation architecture;
\item demonstrate integrated SMR+CCS operation and high-temperature heat transport at relevant duty;
\item secure a cross-border CO$_2$ storage chain and verified T\&S commercial terms;
\item establish Singapore nuclear regulatory/siting readiness and an electricity/heat offtake plan for the remaining reactor output;
\item proceed to front-end engineering only if the mature-design economic assumptions survive project-specific costing.
\end{enumerate}

This roadmap is intentionally conditional: the model result does not establish that these deployment prerequisites are presently satisfied.

\section{Proposed system}\label{sec:proposed-system}

Read the process diagram from left to right: methane and steam enter the reformer; water-gas shift converts carbon monoxide with steam into additional hydrogen and carbon dioxide; an amine system captures much of the carbon dioxide; pressure-swing adsorption purifies the hydrogen. The nuclear reactor changes the heat source, not the underlying conservation of carbon or hydrogen.

The research question becomes physical in this section: what equipment changes when fired reformer heat is replaced by high-temperature nuclear heat? Figures~\ref{fig:smr-explainer}--\ref{fig:proposed-orientation} make the argument in three steps: the current chemical pathway, a reference HTGR energy pathway, and the proposed integration. Detailed numerical design bases and equations follow only after the plant can be understood as a flow of heat and material.

\subsection{Reference chemical process: SMR-H$_2$ with CCS}
Conventional steam methane reforming uses natural gas in two roles. Methane is the chemical feedstock, while a fired furnace supplies the high-temperature reformer duty. Natural gas and steam enter the reformer and leave as hydrogen-rich synthesis gas containing carbon monoxide. Water-Gas Shift (WGS) then converts carbon monoxide with steam toward additional H$_2$ and CO$_2$. The concentrated carbon stream is separated for Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS), while Pressure Swing Adsorption (PSA) recovers hydrogen from the remaining hydrogen-rich gas~\cite{ieaghg2017smr,inltev953}. Thus process carbon originates from methane chemistry, while the fired heat source adds combustion emissions. Figure~\ref{fig:smr-explainer} shows this reference before nuclear heat is introduced.

\subsection{Reference HTGR energy pathway}
A High-Temperature Gas-Cooled Reactor (HTGR) produces heat by fission in its fuel and transfers core heat to circulating primary helium. Figure~\ref{fig:htgr-power-orientation} uses a conventional electricity-generation arrangement only to show what primary helium does: it carries reactor heat to a heat-transfer component and returns to the core in a closed nuclear circuit. A separate steam/water circuit can then drive a turbine-generator. This orientation figure assigns no electricity output to the CN4252 project; the proposed configuration changes where useful reactor heat is sent.

\subsection{Proposed HTGR-assisted SMR-H$_2$+CCS configuration}
The proposed system keeps methane as the hydrogen feedstock and keeps the reforming, WGS, CO$_2$-capture and PSA sequence. The change is the source of high-temperature process heat. Reactor primary helium carries fission heat to an Intermediate Heat Exchanger (IHX). Heat crosses the IHX wall into a separate secondary-helium loop. The secondary helium then travels to the chemical plant, supplies the reformer duty, cools, and returns toward the IHX for reheating. Primary reactor helium therefore does \emph{not} flow through the reformer, and chemical process gas does not enter the reactor primary circuit.

The source-backed operating orientation is a 925$^\circ$C reactor-outlet case feeding 900$^\circ$C secondary helium at 78.49 kg/s; the secondary helium returns at approximately 466$^\circ$C after supplying 176.8 MWth to the reforming process, whose reported outlet is 871$^\circ$C~\cite{inltev953,inltev961}. The reactor scale is a 600 MWth GTHTR300C-class \emph{design basis}, not an operating 600 MWth project~\cite{jaeri2004gthtr300c,nishihara2007potential}. The published approximately 170 MWth physical-IHX reference and approximately 370/371 MWth source process-heat branch are different quantities: the latter is not the rating of one exchanger~\cite{jaeri2004gthtr300c,nishihara2007potential}. The project duty of 176.8 MWth therefore remains subject to exchanger qualification. Likewise, the project-derived 423.2 MWth remainder from the 600 MWth reactor basis is only remaining thermal capacity; it is not electricity output, turbine input, cooling duty or waste heat.

\subsection{Physical operation from reactor heat to products}
The proposed plant can be followed as one continuous heat-and-material sequence:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Make and move reactor heat.} Fission heats the reactor fuel and core. Primary helium removes that heat and carries it to the IHX.
\item \textbf{Cross the nuclear/process-heat interface.} Heat crosses the IHX wall into secondary helium. The two helium streams remain separate circuits; the IHX is a heat-transfer boundary, not evidence that all radiological or safety questions are solved.
\item \textbf{Deliver reformer heat.} Hot secondary helium travels to the reformer heat-transfer surface, supplies the high-temperature duty, and returns cooler toward the IHX.
\item \textbf{Reform methane and shift carbon monoxide.} Natural gas and steam undergo steam methane reforming to a hydrogen-rich synthesis gas. WGS then converts CO with steam toward additional H$_2$ and CO$_2$.
\item \textbf{Separate carbon and hydrogen.} CO$_2$ capture removes the represented concentrated carbon stream. PSA then recovers the hydrogen product; the source 88\% value is recovery, not purity~\cite{inltev953}. Residual/tail-gas handling remains part of the source process boundary and is not re-designed in this conceptual walkthrough.
\item \textbf{Send products to their boundaries.} Hydrogen leaves as product. Captured CO$_2$ is conditioned/compressed for transport toward geological storage, but the actual cross-border route, storage provider and operating availability remain conditional deployment inputs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What is established and what remains conditional}
The configuration deliberately combines evidence of different maturity. HTTR operation demonstrates the HTGR high-temperature class, and INL source modelling supplies the selected SMR-H$_2$ process states~\cite{jaeahttr,inltev953,inltev961}. GTHTR300C literature supplies a commercial-scale high-temperature reactor/process-heat \emph{design basis}~\cite{jaeri2004gthtr300c,nishihara2007potential}. The specific 600 MWth-class reactor + 176.8 MWth INL reforming duty + CCS combination is the project's screening integration; it is not a commercially demonstrated plant. Project-scale IHX qualification, integrated transients and safety, a Singapore nuclear site/licensing case, and an operating cross-border CO$_2$ storage chain remain conditional.

The physical plant is now defined well enough to ask the next quantitative question: which source values, design choices, assumptions and accounting boundaries specify the calculation?

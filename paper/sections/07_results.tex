\section{Results}
The central Gate-5 experiment evaluates 64 physically paired nuclear-assisted cases spanning reformer temperature and pressure, PSA recovery, capture fraction, upstream/auxiliary-carbon conditions and heat/fixed-cost conditions. No case satisfies both CN4252 thresholds simultaneously:
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{joint\ pass}}=0\quad\text{of}\quad N=64.
\label{eq:joint-pass}
\end{equation}
The conservative credible case produces non-positive lifecycle abatement and therefore fails both the annual-abatement and economic criteria.

Figure~\ref{fig:threshold-map} is generated directly from the canonical Rust-produced Gate-5 CSV during every paper build. The dashed lines are the assignment thresholds, so the upper-left region is the joint-pass region: annual avoided emissions greater than \(0.25\) MtCO2e/y and forward abatement cost below S\$100/tCO2e. Infinite-cost cases, which arise when lifecycle abatement is non-positive, remain explicit as \texttt{inf} in the canonical scatter CSV. A separate build-generated finite-cost view is used only for the scatter layer, so those falsification states are excluded from the finite ordinate rather than clipped or assigned an arbitrary cost; their threshold-failure classifications remain included in Table~\ref{tab:binding-counts}.

\subsection{Lifecycle-emissions decomposition}
The reference screening case uses the declared R3 lifecycle corner (upstream natural gas \(11.5~\mathrm{gCO_2e/MJ}\), nuclear electricity-LCA proxy input \(5.5~\mathrm{gCO_2e/kWh_e}\), low-carbon auxiliary electricity \(5.5~\mathrm{gCO_2e/kWh}\), and a CCS-transport burden equal to \(2.5\%\) of captured CO$_2$). These are screening inputs, not Singapore measurements. The nuclear-heat term is especially limited: it converts an electricity lifecycle factor to a thermal-service allocation proxy using the declared reactor efficiency, so it is a bounded estimate rather than a published process-heat LCA.

Figure~\ref{fig:lifecycle-decomposition} shows the resulting component contributions. The plotted total is required by Rust regression tests to equal the sum of residual direct carbon, upstream natural gas, nuclear-heat proxy, auxiliary electricity and CCS-transport contributions.

This decomposition explains why high stack-capture performance alone cannot establish lifecycle compliance: residual plant carbon is only one term in the candidate intensity. Upstream gas supply, energy-related burdens and the CCS chain remain inside the declared lifecycle boundary.

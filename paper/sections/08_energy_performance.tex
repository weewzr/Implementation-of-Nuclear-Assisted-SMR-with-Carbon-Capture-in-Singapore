\section{Energy performance}\label{sec:energy-performance}
The relevant energy question is how much high-temperature heat reaches the two reformers, not how much nominal reactor capacity remains. Each literature-anchored Case-6 train requires 176.8 MWth; the preferred two-train architecture therefore demands 353.6 MWth. This is 95.6\% of the rounded 370 MWth source process-heat branch and 58.9\% of the 600 MWth reactor thermal rating (Section~\ref{sec:design-basis}).

At the conditional full-power point, the identity
\[
600-353.6=246.4~\mathrm{MW_{th}}
\]
defines thermal power not assigned to the reformers. It is a \emph{residual-disposition envelope}, not an operating cooling duty, decay-heat load, turbine input or electricity-export estimate. The reactor could instead operate at a different power point or allocate heat to other qualified sinks. Neither operating strategy is validated by the present off-design model.

A separate component question remains: the published approximately 170 MWth reference physical IHX does not by itself qualify a 176.8 MWth per-train interface, and the 370/371 MWth process-heat branch is not a single physical exchanger. Exchanger/manifold configuration, thermal stresses, tritium transport, part-load control and independent decay-heat removal must be established before energy performance can be treated as a plant design.

The earlier one-train identity, $600-176.8=423.2$ MWth, is retained elsewhere only as historical model-development provenance. No project-specific net electricity output, heat-recovery efficiency or exergy efficiency is claimed.

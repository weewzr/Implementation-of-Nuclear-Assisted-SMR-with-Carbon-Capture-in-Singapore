\section{Conclusions}
The verified quantitative foundation remains unchanged: the INL/NGNP high-temperature nuclear-assisted SMR+CCS case produces 130 MMSCFD H$_2$ (approximately 97,946 t/y at 85\% availability), requires 176.8 MWth process heat and yields approximately 0.917 MtCO$_2$e/y lifecycle abatement~\cite{inltev953,inltev961}. The controlling zero-electricity-revenue economic screen is approximately S\$3.725/tCO$_2$e. Thus the model exceeds the CN4252 0.25 MtCO$_2$e/y requirement by about 3.7 times and remains well below the S\$100/tCO$_2$e ceiling.

The targeted feasibility extension changes the interpretation, not those verified numbers. Temperature compatibility is supported by INL modelling and HTTR's demonstrated 950$^\circ$C operation~\cite{jaeahttr2026ops}. The selected 600 MWth / 370 MWth heat-branch architecture is a design basis, not an operating commercial plant. HTTR safety tests, TRISO qualification evidence and a constructed high-temperature helium--helium IHX provide relevant engineering precedent~\cite{takamatsu2011lofc,inlagrrelease,hamamoto2006ihx}, but they do not constitute a site-specific safety case for a Singapore nuclear/chemical complex.

Overall feasibility is therefore multi-dimensional. \textbf{Thermodynamic/process feasibility and the CN4252 numerical model thresholds are supported.} Project-scale IHX/materials integration, coupled transients and nuclear/process safety are \textbf{conditional on further engineering}. Singapore nuclear licensing/siting and an operating cross-border CCS service remain \textbf{unresolved deployment dependencies}; Singapore has not decided to deploy nuclear energy~\cite{mti2026inir,mti2026safetycriteria,mti2026carbon}. Bankable FOAK project economics are \textbf{not demonstrated}.

Accordingly, the final answer is an independently verified \textbf{CONDITIONAL MODEL PASS}, not demonstrated commercial, regulatory or safety feasibility. A deployment decision would require the staged evidence, regulatory, CCS, component-qualification, integrated-safety and project-development gates set out in Section 10.

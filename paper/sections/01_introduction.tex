\section{Introduction and research question}
The CN4252 problem asks for a Singapore-scale solution capable of reducing more than 0.25 MtCO$_2$e/y at less than S\$100/tCO$_2$e, with a clear abatement mechanism and implementation roadmap. This paper evaluates whether high-temperature nuclear heat can replace fired reformer duty in an industrial steam-methane-reforming (SMR) hydrogen plant while CCS addresses feedstock carbon.

The final research question is: \emph{does the strongest directly supported HTGR-assisted SMR+CCS design found in the primary literature satisfy both CN4252 thresholds when its process balance is extended to a transparent Singapore lifecycle and forward-economic boundary?}

The final process basis is not the project's earlier fixed-scale screen. It is INL's NGNP HTGR-integrated SMR analysis, which directly models nuclear process heat, SMR chemistry and carbon capture~\cite{inltev953,inltev961}. JAEA/JAERI evidence is used for the HTGR/IHX/cogeneration hardware and mature one-module economics. Earlier screening configurations remain in the repository as reproducible design-development evidence.

\section{Introduction and research question}
The CN4252 problem asks for a Singapore-scale solution capable of reducing more than 0.25 MtCO2e/y at less than S\$100/tCO2e, with a clear abatement mechanism and implementation roadmap. The candidate hypothesis investigated here is that high-temperature nuclear heat, represented by an HTGR-class scenario, could replace fossil reformer heat and support CCS in an SMR hydrogen plant.

The research question is deliberately falsifiable: \emph{under a common H2 production basis and explicit lifecycle/economic boundaries, does nuclear-assisted SMR+CCS satisfy both CN4252 thresholds over a defensible uncertainty domain?} The work also tests whether conventional SMR+CCS or other alternatives remain credible comparators rather than assuming a nuclear configuration is preferable.

The common production basis is the IEAGHG merchant-hydrogen reference~\cite{ieaghg2017smr} of 100,000 Nm3/h H2 (8,994 kg/h), operated for 8,322 h/y. Specific results use kgCO2e/kgH2 before annual scaling.

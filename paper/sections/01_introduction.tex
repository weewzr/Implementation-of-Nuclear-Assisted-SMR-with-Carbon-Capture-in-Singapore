\section{Introduction and research question}
Singapore's CN4252 project asks for a solution that can avoid more than 0.25 MtCO$_2$e/y at less than S\$100/tCO$_2$e. This study tests one industrial decarbonisation concept against that screen: producing hydrogen by Steam Methane Reforming (SMR) with Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS), while replacing the reformer's fired high-temperature heat with nuclear process heat.

Conventional SMR has two distinct carbon problems. Natural gas is the hydrogen feedstock, so carbon enters the chemistry with methane. The reformer also needs high-temperature heat, conventionally supplied by combustion. CCS can remove much of the concentrated process CO$_2$, but it does not by itself remove the fired heat requirement or upstream natural-gas emissions. The proposed system therefore changes the heat source while retaining the SMR chemistry and adding capture of the process-carbon stream.

A High-Temperature Gas-Cooled Reactor (HTGR) is relevant because helium can carry heat at the temperatures required by the reformer. The proposed plant does not send reactor coolant into the hydrogen process. An Intermediate Heat Exchanger (IHX) transfers heat from the reactor's primary helium loop to a separate secondary-helium loop; that secondary loop supplies heat to the reformer.

The research question is: \emph{under the adopted literature-anchored process, reactor, lifecycle and economic model, can an HTGR-assisted SMR+CCS system supply the required reformer heat and satisfy the CN4252 emissions and abatement-cost thresholds?}

The analysis follows that question in physical order. First, the proposed configuration and system boundary are defined. Next, the reactor-to-reformer heat path is checked using INL process states and a GTHTR300C-class design basis~\cite{inltev953,inltev961,nishihara2007potential}. The source hydrogen rate is then annualised, the carbon ledger is constructed, and the represented annual costs are compared with the conventional baseline. Existing sensitivity screens test robustness. Only after the model result is established does the paper address the unresolved Singapore deployment questions: component qualification, nuclear/chemical safety, siting, regulation and cross-border CO$_2$ storage.

The model is intentionally described according to its provenance. It combines literature-derived process/reactor parameters, fixed design bases, declared screening assumptions, engineering balances and derived Rust calculations. It is not presented as a wholly first-principles plant simulation.

Third, nuclear/chemical isolation is finite rather than absolute.  Primary helium does not flow directly into the chemical plant, but measured HTTR tritium in secondary helium shows that radionuclide permeation must be assessed.  Likewise, Japanese process-trip experiments support thermal buffering/control but do not replace a project-scale transient model.

Fourth, emergency planning is a consequence-and-policy result rather than a generic advanced-reactor radius.  Chinese HTR-PM research supports much smaller technical EPZs than conventional large-reactor practice, yet the implemented Chinese HTR-PM emergency plan retained kilometre-scale plume zones.  Singapore therefore requires its own sequence/frequency/source-term/dispersion/dose/protective-action analysis.

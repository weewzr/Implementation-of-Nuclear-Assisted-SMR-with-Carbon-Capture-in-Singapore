# Nuclear premium / required-savings test

## Why this test is needed

The Singapore-adjusted conventional Case-1A screening comparator is approximately S$146-180/t direct CO2 avoided under the current Group-A T&S and process-cost escalation assumptions.

The authoritative assignment target is <S$100/tCO2e.

Therefore adding a nuclear system cannot be economically justified merely by saying it removes furnace gas. Relative to this comparator, nuclear integration must either:

1. lower annual net cost enough to offset its own annualised cost; and/or
2. increase lifecycle CO2e abatement enough to enlarge the denominator.

## Fixed-denominator diagnostic

IEAGHG Case 1A direct plant avoidance:

(0.8091 - 0.3704) kg/Nm3
x 100,000 Nm3/h
x 8322 h/y
= approximately 365,100 tCO2/y avoided.

If the denominator were held fixed at that direct-plant value, moving:

S$146/t -> S$100/t

requires annual net savings of approximately:

(146-100)*365,100
~= S$16.8m/y.

Moving:

S$180/t -> S$100/t

requires approximately:

S$29.2m/y.

Thus the nuclear-assisted architecture would need to generate of order **S$17-29m/y of net savings** before any positive nuclear premium could be tolerated under this fixed-denominator diagnostic.

This is not the final CN4252 calculation because nuclear integration changes lifecycle emissions and may change capture fraction, increasing the denominator.

## Potential savings channels to test

Real resource/economic savings:
- avoided supplementary furnace NG;
- lower conventional utility consumption;
- reduced capture-energy penalty if nuclear heat is integrated effectively;
- valuable electricity/steam cogeneration;
- possible process-intensification savings;
- avoided conventional furnace equipment/replacement CAPEX.

Costs:
- allocated HTGR CAPEX/O&M/fuel;
- IHX;
- secondary-He loop;
- reformer modification;
- tail-gas recycle/treatment;
- helium circulator electricity;
- nuclear licensing/security/safety systems;
- additional financing/construction risk.

Policy/cash-flow effects:
- avoided Singapore carbon tax.

The carbon tax must be reported separately from resource abatement cost. It can improve the private business case, but counting an avoided tax as a societal resource saving would double-count the carbon externality if the assignment metric is interpreted socially.

## Singapore carbon-tax context

NEA confirms:
- S$45/tCO2e for 2026-2027;
- view of S$50-80/t by 2030;
- eligible international credits can offset up to 5% of taxable emissions.

At the Case-1A direct-plant avoided scale, S$45/t corresponds to a private avoided-tax cash flow of order S$16m/y if all avoided emissions were taxable and no allowances/other adjustments applied.

This is comparable in magnitude to the lower required-savings diagnostic, which means policy treatment can materially affect private economics.

However, the project will maintain two economic views:
1. **resource/abatement cost** excluding tax transfers;
2. **private project cash flow** including applicable carbon-tax savings/incentives.

## Competing economic hypothesis

H0-economic:
Conventional SMR+CCS under Singapore cross-border T&S already exceeds S$100/tCO2e, and adding nuclear cannot generate enough real savings to overcome its incremental capital.

H1-economic:
Direct nuclear heat/cogeneration increases abatement and/or avoids enough fuel/utility cost that the net incremental cost per lifecycle tonne falls below S$100.

The next modelling work should calculate the largest real savings channels first, beginning with avoided furnace NG and electricity/steam changes, before detailed nuclear CAPEX is imposed.

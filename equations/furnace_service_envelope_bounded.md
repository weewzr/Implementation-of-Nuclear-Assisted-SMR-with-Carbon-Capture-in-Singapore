# Bounded conventional furnace-service envelope

## Why a bound is preferable to a guessed split

IEAGHG states that saturated HP steam at 4.23 MPa is generated by three sources:

1. Reformer Waste Heat Boiler;
2. Shift Converter Waste Heat Recovery;
3. Convective Section Steam Generator.

It states that **around 75%** is generated by the Reformer Waste Heat Boiler, but does not publish the split of the remaining ~25% between items 2 and 3 in the accessible summary data. citeturn0search25turn0search26

Therefore this project does not invent that split.

## Steam-generation upper bound

The combined process/export HP steam scale is approximately:

m_HP = 141.354 t/h.

The non-syngas-WHB group is approximately:

m_non-WHB ~= 0.25 * 141.354
          ~= 35.34 t/h.

For saturated steam near the source pressure, steam-table latent heats bracket approximately:

h_fg(4.0 MPa) = 1713.3 kJ/kg
h_fg(4.5 MPa) = 1675.6 kJ/kg.

The source pressure 4.23 MPa lies between them.

If **all** of the non-WHB steam were assigned to the furnace convection coil, the corresponding vaporisation duty would be approximately:

Q_furnace-SG,max ~= 16.45-16.82 MW.

This is intentionally an upper bound because IEAGHG explicitly says the Shift Converter Waste Heat Recovery also generates saturated steam. The true furnace-coil contribution must be smaller. citeturn0search25

## First bounded furnace-service result

The current calculated service lower envelope is:

- 600 C reformer-inlet sensitivity: 130.74-130.97 MW;
- 625 C: 132.48-132.71 MW;
- 650 C: 134.23-134.46 MW.

Adding the deliberately conservative furnace-SG upper bound gives an overall current bounding logic of approximately:

- low end: ~130.7 MW;
- high end: ~151.3 MW.

Thus, for the currently represented conventional-furnace services:

**Q_furnace-services ~ 131-151 MWth**

at the 100,000 Nm3/h H2 reference scale.

## Interpretation

The lower side omits the furnace steam-generation contribution and retains conservative pre-reformer assumptions.

The upper side assigns the entire non-syngas-WHB steam-generation remainder to the furnace even though some is demonstrably supplied by shift heat recovery.

The true value should therefore lie inside a narrower interval once the shift-WHB duty is recovered.

This range still does not include nuclear IHX/secondary-loop heat losses. Electrical helium-circulator parasitics are kept separate.

## Why the shift split is no longer a hard blocker

The unresolved shift/furnace steam split can move the service estimate by at most roughly 17 MW under this bounding method.

That is now a quantified uncertainty rather than an unbounded missing term.

This is sufficient to begin a first common-service comparison between direct nuclear heat and nuclear-electric reforming while continuing to refine the steam split in parallel.

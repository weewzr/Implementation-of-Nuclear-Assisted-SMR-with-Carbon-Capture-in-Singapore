# Conventional steam-service ledger

## Source-derived mass flows

IEAGHG 2017-02 base-case heat/material balance reports:

- HP steam to process: 95,301 kg/h at 400 C and 4.29 MPa;
- HP steam export: 46,053 kg/h at 395 C and 4.23 MPa.

Therefore the hydrogen plant handles approximately:

m_HP,total = 141,354 kg/h = 141.354 t/h

of high-pressure superheated steam across these two principal destinations.

IEAGHG's process description states that saturated HP steam is generated through:
1. reformer syngas waste-heat boiler;
2. shift-converter waste-heat recovery;
3. furnace-convection steam-generator coil;

and that around 75% is generated in the reformer syngas waste-heat boiler.

The saturated steam is then sent to the furnace steam-superheater coil to approximately 395 C before being divided between reformer/pre-reformer process use and export/cogeneration.

## Retained versus lost services under nuclear heating

If a nuclear-heated reformer preserves comparable reformer-product temperature and cooling:

### Potentially retained
The syngas waste-heat boiler remains available.

Approximate retained steam-generation scale:

0.75 * 141.354 ~= 106.0 t/h.

This is an approximate allocation because IEAGHG says "around 75%" and the steam system includes blowdown/conditioning details.

### Not attributable uniquely yet
The remaining approximately:

0.25 * 141.354 ~= 35.3 t/h

is generated by the **combination** of shift heat recovery and the furnace-convection steam-generator coil.

Therefore 35.3 t/h is an upper-group bound, NOT the amount lost when the furnace disappears. Some of it is generated by the shift section and remains recoverable.

### Furnace-dependent superheating
The source description routes the saturated steam through the furnace steam-superheater coil before process/export use.

Therefore furnace removal creates a superheating-service requirement for approximately the full 141 t/h HP steam flow unless the steam system is redesigned.

This is a stronger and more important constraint than assuming the furnace contributes only the non-WHB steam-generation fraction.

## Thermodynamic-property method

Steam heat duties will be computed from:

Q = m_dot (h_out - h_in)

using IAPWS/NIST water-steam properties.

NISTIR 5078 tabulates enthalpy, entropy, density and related water/steam properties from IAPWS-95 and is the authoritative property basis selected for this model.

No numerical superheat duty is recorded yet because the exact saturated-steam inlet state to the superheater must be evaluated consistently at the source pressure.

## Revised nuclear service vector

For the IEAGHG reference scale:

1. High-temperature reformer radiant duty:
   Q_radiant = 96.04 MW (verified).

2. HP steam superheat:
   m_dot ~= 141.354 t/h, from saturation to ~395-400 C.
   Q_superheat = pending IAPWS calculation.

3. Furnace-convection steam generation:
   unknown individually, but bounded above by the non-syngas-WHB group of ~35.3 t/h because shift heat recovery shares that remainder.

4. Feed/pre-reformer/reformer preheat:
   source confirms these coils exist; individual duties remain unresolved.

5. Syngas WHB steam generation:
   approximately 75% of saturated HP steam generation and potentially retained under nuclear heating.

## Important architecture implication

JAEA's nuclear steam-reforming architecture includes a steam reformer, steam superheater and steam generator in the secondary-helium loop. This is now seen not merely as a historical design choice but as directly aligned with the services lost when the conventional fired furnace is removed.

The next calculation should quantify Q_superheat from IAPWS properties, then bound the remaining furnace-convection steam-generation and feed-preheat duties.

# Conventional SMR + CCS baseline: first mathematical foundation

## Purpose

This is the first screening-level baseline. It is deliberately separated from the nuclear model so that the marginal value of nuclear heat can be measured against a reproducible counterfactual.

## Reaction stoichiometry

Primary steam methane reforming:

$$
\mathrm{CH_4 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons CO + 3H_2}
$$

Water-gas shift:

$$
\mathrm{CO + H_2O \rightleftharpoons CO_2 + H_2}
$$

The ideal combined reaction is:

$$
\mathrm{CH_4 + 2H_2O \rightarrow CO_2 + 4H_2}.
$$

DOE describes SMR as a mature endothermic process using high-temperature steam, followed by water-gas shift and PSA purification. Typical SMR operation is described as 700-1000 C and 3-25 bar. citeturn535804search2

## Ideal stoichiometric bounds

Let $M_i$ be molecular mass in kg/kmol.

For complete conversion according to the combined reaction:

$$
\left(\frac{m_{CH_4}}{m_{H_2}}\right)_{stoich}
=
\frac{M_{CH_4}}{4M_{H_2}}
\approx 1.990.
$$

The corresponding ideal process CO2 formation is:

$$
\left(\frac{m_{CO_2}}{m_{H_2}}\right)_{stoich}
=
\frac{M_{CO_2}}{4M_{H_2}}
\approx 5.46.
$$

These are thermochemical stoichiometric bounds, not a realistic plant-performance prediction.

## Feed and product scaling

For a plant producing $\dot m_{H_2}$ kg H2/h:

$$
\dot m_{CH_4}=s_{NG}\dot m_{H_2}
$$

where $s_{NG}$ is the measured/modelled natural-gas consumption in kg NG/kg H2.

Published Aspen-based SMR modelling gives, for one 500 t/d case:

$$
s_{NG}=3.16\ \mathrm{kg\ NG/kg\ H_2}
$$

and

$$
\dot m_{H_2O}=9.74\ \mathrm{kg\ H_2O/kg\ H_2},
$$

with a plant-level direct CO2 emission intensity of approximately:

$$
e_{CO_2}=8.47\ \mathrm{kg\ CO_2/kg\ H_2}.
$$

That publication models a 20,830 kg/h (500 t/d) plant using Aspen Plus and reports 90% H2 PSA recovery and 99.99% product purity. citeturn869272search0

These values are retained as a **benchmark case**, not adopted as universal constants.

## CO2 split

The plant must distinguish at least:

1. process CO2 generated by reforming + WGS;
2. combustion CO2 from furnace fuel;
3. upstream natural-gas supply-chain emissions;
4. emissions associated with capture energy;
5. downstream CO2 conditioning/transport/storage emissions.

NETL/DOE and IEA sources distinguish the concentrated syngas/process stream from reformer flue gas and PSA-related streams. citeturn535804search61turn535804search60

For capture streams $j$:

$$
\dot m_{CO_2,captured}
=
\sum_j \eta_j\dot m_{CO_2,j}.
$$

Do not use a single capture percentage for all streams unless the model explicitly proves that the same fraction is appropriate.

## Lifecycle emissions

Define:

$$
e_{LCA}
=
e_{NG,upstream}
+
e_{NG,plant}
+
e_{electricity}
+
e_{CCS}
+
e_{transport/storage}
+
e_{nuclear}
$$

with terms included only when the declared boundary requires them.

IEA reports 10-12 kgCO2e/kgH2 for unabated natural-gas hydrogen and explicitly warns that upstream and midstream emissions remain material after point-source capture. citeturn535804search0turn535804search3

## Abatement relative to a baseline

For a candidate configuration $k$ and counterfactual baseline $b$:

$$
\Delta e_k=e_b-e_k
$$

$$
\dot m_{CO_2e,avoided,k}
=
\dot m_{H_2,k}\Delta e_k.
$$

Annual abatement:

$$
A_k=
\dot m_{H_2,k}
\Delta e_k
t_{op}
\times 10^{-6}
$$

where $A_k$ is MtCO2e/y when mass units are kg and $t_{op}$ is operating time in h/y.

## CN4252 scale threshold

The assignment requires:

$$
A_k>0.25\ \mathrm{MtCO_2e/y}.
$$

Therefore the required hydrogen production is:

$$
\dot M_{H_2,annual}
>
\frac{0.25\times10^6\ \mathrm{t\ CO_2e/y}}
{\Delta e_k\ \mathrm{t\ CO_2e/t\ H_2}}
=
\frac{250}{\Delta e_k}\ \mathrm{kt\ H_2/y}
$$

when $\Delta e_k$ is expressed in kgCO2e/kgH2.

Examples:

| Marginal abatement | H2 required for 0.25 MtCO2e/y |
|---:|---:|
| 2 kgCO2e/kgH2 | 125 kt/y |
| 3 kgCO2e/kgH2 | 83.3 kt/y |
| 5 kgCO2e/kgH2 | 50.0 kt/y |
| 7 kgCO2e/kgH2 | 35.7 kt/y |
| 10 kgCO2e/kgH2 | 25.0 kt/y |

This is a scaling identity, not a prediction. The required capacity rises rapidly as the marginal emissions reduction shrinks.

## Cost of abatement

The eventual assignment metric should be based on incremental economics:

$$
C_{abatement}
=
\frac{C_k-C_b}
{A_k-A_b}
$$

on a common annualised basis.

A final definition must specify:
- CAPEX treatment;
- financing / discount rate;
- operating period;
- fuel prices;
- electricity price;
- nuclear cost allocation;
- CCS transport/storage cost;
- plant utilization;
- currency year.

The numerator must represent the incremental cost of the candidate relative to its actual counterfactual, not total hydrogen production cost alone.

## Current scientific finding
Direct nuclear process heat for steam methane reforming is an established research area. The useful contribution is therefore not inventing nuclear-assisted reforming, but evaluating it under a Singapore-specific system boundary and quantitative CN4252 criteria.

JAEA reports a planned HTTR demonstration coupling a 950 C HTGR to methane steam reforming, while noting that nuclear-to-hydrogen plant coupling has not yet been achieved.

## Current bottleneck
Establish a defensible conventional SMR baseline:
- material flows and hydrogen yield
- reformer heat demand
- process/combustion CO2 split
- CCS capture boundary and regeneration duty
- CO2 compression
- lifecycle emissions boundary
- production scale required for the CN4252 abatement target

## Next scientific step
Complete a literature-backed baseline mass/energy/carbon model before implementing the nuclear integration model.

## Important unresolved questions
1. What reactor temperature range is genuinely compatible with direct reformer heating?
2. How much reactor heat is displaced versus merely transferred through an intermediate loop?
3. Does direct nuclear heat outperform nuclear electricity plus eSMR after conversion losses?
4. Does CCS dominate the emissions reduction enough to make nuclear heat integration marginal?
5. Can methane-derived hydrogen meet the lifecycle carbon-intensity target once upstream methane emissions are included?
6. Can the required production scale and infrastructure plausibly fit Singapore?
7. What cost assumptions are required to approach S$100/tCO2e, and how uncertain are they?

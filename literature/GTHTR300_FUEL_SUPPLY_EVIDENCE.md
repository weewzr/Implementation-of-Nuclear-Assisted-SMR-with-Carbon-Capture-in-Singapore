# GTHTR300 Fuel Supply / HALEU Evidence

## Question

Does the selected GTHTR300C-class architecture depend on HALEU, enrichment/fabrication capacity and a fuel supply chain that Singapore does not currently possess?

## Fuel specification

Japanese/IAEA GTHTR300 references identify:
- UO2 TRISO fuel;
- average U-235 enrichment about **14 wt%**;
- published GTHTR300C enrichment zoning can span approximately **7.0-16.8%** depending core position/design;
- GTHTR300 reference average ~14%.

Under the widely used definition of HALEU as >5% and <20% U-235, this is **HALEU-class fuel**.

Therefore the selected reactor architecture is not supplied by ordinary ~3-5% LWR enrichment alone.

## Japanese fabrication evidence

Japan has genuine HTGR fuel-fabrication experience:
- Nuclear Fuel Industries/JAEA developed HTGR coated-particle fabrication;
- a ~400 kgU/y commercial-scale HTGR fuel plant was constructed in Japan in 1992;
- HTTR initial and second-loading fuels were manufactured, totalling roughly 2 t uranium.

However, HTTR enrichment was approximately 3.4-9.9%, not the full 14% GTHTR300 commercial specification.

Japanese research on 14 wt% GTHTR300 fuel explicitly identifies **more severe criticality-safety constraints in fabrication** and investigates burnable-poison credit to ease fabrication limitations.

Evidence class:
**HTGR FUEL FABRICATION DEMONSTRATED; 14% COMMERCIAL GTHTR300 SUPPLY NOT DEMONSTRATED.**

## Current Japanese HALEU development status

Japan's Agency for Natural Resources and Energy launched a 2025 HTGR demonstration-reactor R&D programme specifically for **fuel enrichment**, noting that advanced HTGRs are considering HALEU and that enrichment R&D is needed for demonstration-reactor development.

This is direct native policy evidence that fuel enrichment remains an active supply-chain development issue.

OECD-NEA's GTHTR300 assessment similarly identifies fuel as a deployment gap and notes limited/non-established commercial qualified HALEU supply in OECD markets at the time of assessment.

## Singapore consequence

Singapore has no domestic uranium enrichment or TRISO fuel-fabrication industry in the current project evidence.

A deployment pathway would therefore require:
- international HALEU procurement;
- conversion/fuel-kernel supply;
- qualified TRISO coating/compact/block fabrication;
- safeguards/material accountancy;
- transport/security licensing;
- fresh-fuel storage;
- vendor qualification;
- long-term supply diversification.

This is not necessarily a technical show-stopper, but it is a **strategic supply-chain dependency**.

## Non-proliferation / safeguards

14% U-235 remains below 20% and is low-enriched uranium, but:
- enrichment is substantially higher than conventional LWR fuel;
- criticality controls are more demanding;
- safeguards/security and transport arrangements remain mandatory.

The project should use “HALEU-class LEU” or “~14% enriched LEU/HALEU” rather than implying highly enriched uranium.

## Decision consequence

If qualified 14%-class HALEU/TRISO fuel cannot be procured under acceptable safeguards, cost and geopolitical conditions:

**GTHTR300C-CLASS DEPLOYMENT IS BLOCKED UNTIL A QUALIFIED FUEL SUPPLY CHAIN EXISTS**, or the reactor/fuel design must change.

A different enrichment/fuel design would require a new reactor-physics/safety/economic basis; it cannot be substituted casually.

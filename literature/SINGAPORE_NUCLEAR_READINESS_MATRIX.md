# Singapore Nuclear Regulatory and Infrastructure Readiness Matrix

## Current national status

Singapore has **not decided to deploy nuclear energy**. Starting in 2027 it plans an IAEA INIR Phase-1 mission to assess whether it has the expertise, institutions and frameworks to make an informed decision. The IAEA Milestones framework examines 19 infrastructure issues.

Therefore this project's regulatory conclusion is:

**SINGAPORE NUCLEAR DEPLOYMENT READINESS = UNRESOLVED / PRE-DECISION CAPABILITY-BUILDING STAGE.**

A technically feasible HTGR process does not close this gate.

## Important distinction: current radiation regulation != power-reactor licensing readiness

Singapore already has real nuclear/radiation governance:
- Radiation Protection Act;
- licensing/inspection of radioactive material and radiation apparatus;
- safeguards implementation;
- radioactive-waste controls;
- radiological emergency functions.

These capabilities are relevant foundations. They do **not** imply that Singapore currently has a complete legal/regulatory framework for licensing, constructing and operating a commercial nuclear power reactor. The planned INIR Phase-1 assessment exists precisely to identify capability/infrastructure gaps.

## Waste implications

The Ionising Radiation Regulations 2023 require supporting safety, environmental, security and emergency-plan information for radioactive-waste approvals and traceability from collection through long-term storage/disposal.

For this project, that means a future HTGR programme would need a much more extensive route for:
- fresh enriched fuel;
- irradiated TRISO/fuel blocks;
- irradiated graphite;
- activated components;
- operational liquid/gaseous/solid waste;
- transport;
- interim storage;
- final disposal/reprocessing;
- decommissioning.

The existing regulatory provisions are a starting legal capability, not the final back-end solution.

## Singapore safety decision criterion

MTI states any deployment decision will consider:
- safety;
- reliability;
- affordability;
- environmental sustainability.

EMA further states safety is an overriding priority because Singapore is a small, densely populated city-state.

This project's decision structure should therefore not reduce feasibility to S$/tCO2e. A deployment candidate would need to pass **all** relevant safety/infrastructure gates even if the CN4252 numerical screen remains favourable.

## Milestone interpretation for this project

### Phase A — informed-decision readiness
Current Singapore stage. Required project contribution:
- credible technology/evidence maturity;
- no unsupported safety claims;
- explicit unresolved site/waste/EPR questions;
- transparent V&V and uncertainty.

### Phase B — project/bid readiness
Only after a national decision. Would require:
- selected reactor/vendor;
- candidate site;
- regulatory/licensing framework;
- owner/operator structure;
- financing/liability;
- fuel/waste strategy;
- detailed safety case;
- environmental assessment;
- CCS contracts for this hybrid project.

### Phase C — commissioning/operation readiness
Would require:
- constructed/qualified plant;
- operator/regulator competence;
- emergency arrangements/exercises;
- safeguards/security;
- waste/fuel logistics;
- commissioning tests and validated performance.

The CN4252 project is a **Phase-A screening research contribution**, not a Phase-B/C project design.

## Current conclusion

- DF-26 Singapore regulatory readiness: **PARTIALLY ANSWERED — current capability building is real, but commercial nuclear-power licensing readiness is unresolved**.
- Professor PF-13 graded regulation: **PARTIALLY ANSWERED — international graded approaches exist; Singapore's future advanced-reactor framework is not established**.
- PF-14 workforce/capability: **ANSWERED AT ROADMAP LEVEL — dedicated teams/INIR exist; deployment workforce remains to be built**.
- Deployment readiness overall: **NOT DEMONSTRATED**.

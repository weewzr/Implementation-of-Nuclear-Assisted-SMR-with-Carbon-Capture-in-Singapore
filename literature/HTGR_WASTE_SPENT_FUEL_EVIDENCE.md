# HTGR Spent-Fuel and Graphite Waste Evidence

## Professor-generated question

**Do HTGR/TRISO spent fuels require the same spent-fuel-pool pathway as conventional LWR fuel, and what waste burden would Singapore inherit?**

## Evidence-based answer

**Pool cooling is not inherently required for every HTGR design.** Dry, passively air-cooled interim storage is an established design route for modern HTGR concepts and has reached commissioning/operational-system hardware in HTR-PM.

**Long-term back-end management is not solved generically.** HTGR fuel contains a large graphite fraction and has reactor-specific storage, packaging, disposal/reprocessing and irradiated-graphite issues. Singapore would still need an approved national or international spent-fuel/waste strategy.

## Evidence ladder

### GTHTR300 / JAEA
JAEA GTHTR300 safety work describes passive air-cooled dry storage without a storage pool as applicable to spent fuel because of the low decay-heat power density. This is **design evidence** for a prismatic reactor family close to the project's selected architecture.

It does not establish a licensed Singapore storage facility or repository.

### HTR-PM
HTR-PM uses continuous pebble discharge and a dedicated system that conveys spent pebbles to the spent-fuel building and loads them into dry canisters for on-site interim storage. Published work describes system design, experiments and commissioning tests.

Separate HTR-PM work describes unshielded steel canisters placed in dry storage silos with a residual-heat-removal system. Drop-analysis work reports full-scale test validation for canister mechanical behaviour.

This is **hardware/commissioning evidence** that a pool is not universally required for modern HTGR spent fuel.

Transferability limit: HTR-PM is pebble-bed fuel; the selected GTHTR300C-class project is prismatic. Packaging geometry, discharge mode and graphite form differ.

### IAEA back-end status
IAEA's 2023/2025 HTGR fuel-cycle work identifies two broad routes:
1. non-dismantling / co-disposal of fuel and graphite;
2. dismantling / separation of fuel-bearing components and graphite.

Open R&D issues include:
- long-term TRISO coating behaviour in groundwater;
- oxidation/corrosion of coating layers and graphite;
- helium pressurisation and coating failure over storage/disposal times;
- long-term fission-product migration;
- reprocessing head-end recovery;
- graphite waste characterisation and disposal.

IAEA explicitly notes that industrial-level back-end technology for HTGR fuels still requires R&D.

### Graphite burden
HTGR waste differs qualitatively from LWR fuel because a large mass/volume of irradiated graphite accompanies a comparatively small fissile kernel inventory. Recent IAEA technical-meeting material states that roughly 92% of spent HTR fuel by the relevant mass/volume context is graphite and that worldwide experience with HTR back-end solutions remains limited.

Long-lived activation products in irradiated graphite, notably C-14 and Cl-36, can control waste classification and disposal strategy. Fuel-bearing graphite and non-fuel moderator/reflector graphite must be distinguished.

## What the project can and cannot claim

### Supported
- HTGR spent fuel can be compatible with dry interim storage; a spent-fuel pool is **not universally required**.
- HTR-PM provides commissioned dry-canister/silo system evidence.
- GTHTR300 design work supports passive dry-storage concepts.
- HTGR back-end management has distinct graphite/TRISO issues.

### Not supported
- that Singapore can omit all wet handling or cooling systems without a selected vendor/fuel-storage design;
- that dry storage solves final disposal;
- that deep borehole disposal is selected or licensed for this project;
- that spent fuel can simply be exported to the fuel supplier;
- that Singapore already has a repository or regulatory waste pathway.

## Singapore feasibility implications

A nuclear newcomer programme would need, before deployment:
- ownership and liability rules for spent fuel and radioactive waste;
- interim storage site and security;
- heat-removal and shielding design;
- safeguards/material-accountancy arrangements;
- transport/package licensing if fuel is moved;
- final disposal/reprocessing policy;
- irradiated-graphite classification and disposal route;
- decommissioning waste strategy.

These are regulatory/institutional deployment gates rather than CN4252 process-model inputs.

# Chinese-Native HTR-PM EPZ / Source-Term Evidence

## Purpose

Resolve DF-17/DF-18 using Chinese regulator, Tsinghua/INET and original HTR-PM evidence. Chinese radii are evidence about **methodology and regulatory implementation in China**; none is transferred to Singapore.

## Four distinct evidence layers

### A. HTR-PM research result — several-hundred-metre / exclusion-boundary scale

Ding, Tong, Wang & Zhang (Annals of Nuclear Energy 111, 2018) develop an HTR-PM-specific EPZ method using the NUREG-0396 technical framework. They identify design-basis accidents and emergency reference release categories based on HTR-PM accident characteristics, then evaluate source/release and emergency consequences. Their research conclusion is that plume- and ingestion-pathway EPZ radii **can be reduced to the exclusion-area boundary, on the order of several hundred metres**, for the analysed HTR-PM design.

Evidence class: **PEER-REVIEWED DESIGN-SPECIFIC RESEARCH RESULT**.

It is not evidence of the emergency zones actually implemented by Chinese authorities.

### B. Actual HTR-PM emergency plan — 3 km / 7 km / 30 km

A 2022 MEE environmental-impact report for the Shidaowan site explicitly cites the HTR-PM on-site nuclear emergency plan (2021 edition) and states that the demonstration plant's emergency planning zones were set at the **lower bounds of the national standard**:

- plume EPZ inner zone: **3 km**;
- plume EPZ outer zone: **7 km**;
- ingestion EPZ: **30 km**.

The report states that actual boundaries also consider terrain, administrative boundaries, population distribution, transport/communications, socioeconomic conditions and public psychology.

Evidence class: **IMPLEMENTED / REGULATORY-PLAN EVIDENCE**.

This is the correct Chinese operational-plan reference and must not be replaced by the smaller research radius.

### C. Chinese small-reactor scalable-EPZ regulatory example

An official MEE environmental-impact document for an integrated small-reactor demonstration quotes the small-reactor EPZ approach: EPZ determination should follow GB/T 17680.1-2008 principles and methods while accounting for small-reactor technical features, accident analysis and integrated factors. A **recommended EPZ range not exceeding 3 km** is given, with the operator required to justify the specific boundary through systematic analysis and scientific calculation.

The same document reports a project-specific analysis supporting a **1 km** EPZ boundary for that small-reactor project under its own accident consequence and dose assessment.

Evidence class: **CHINESE REGULATORY/GUIDANCE EXAMPLE FOR A DIFFERENT SMALL REACTOR**.

It demonstrates scalability when justified. It does not establish an HTR-PM or Singapore radius.

### D. Singapore GTHTR300C-class project — UNKNOWN

No project-specific:
- accident spectrum/frequencies;
- mechanistic source term;
- release fractions;
- atmospheric dispersion;
- candidate-site meteorology;
- off-site dose;
- population/protective-action model;
- Singapore nuclear EPZ criterion

exists at sufficient fidelity.

Therefore:

**PROJECT EPZ = UNKNOWN — REQUIRES SITE-SPECIFIC SAFETY ANALYSIS.**

## Chinese methodology reconstruction

Chinese HTR-PM safety-review and research evidence supports the following chain:

ACCIDENT SEQUENCE
→ FREQUENCY / RISK SIGNIFICANCE
→ RADIONUCLIDE INVENTORY
→ FUEL / CORE RELEASE
→ PRIMARY-CIRCUIT TRANSPORT
→ SOURCE TERM / ENVIRONMENTAL RELEASE
→ ATMOSPHERIC DISPERSION
→ PUBLIC DOSE
→ PROTECTIVE-ACTION / ACCEPTANCE CRITERION
→ EPZ + EMERGENCY PREPARATION.

### Accident sequence / source term
The former Chinese environmental regulator's *HTR-PM Demonstration Plant Safety Review Principles* states that conventional LWR hypothetical source terms are not directly applicable to HTR-PM. HTR-PM accident source terms should instead be determined from radioactive releases caused by **specific accident-sequence analysis**. Important design-basis and beyond-design-basis sequences must be analysed; conservative/bounding source terms should be selected for site and emergency planning. Where mechanisms/data are insufficiently understood, appropriate conservatism is required.

This is a strong native regulatory basis for mechanistic, sequence-specific source terms.

### PSA / PRA
Tsinghua INET's Reactor Safety Research Division identifies PSA/PRA as a core capability and provides technical support to NNSA. Its source-term programme explicitly studies radionuclide generation, migration and release, including HTGR fission products, radioactive dust, normal-operation and accident source terms.

Evidence class: **NATIVE INSTITUTIONAL / REGULATORY-TECHNICAL CAPABILITY**.

### Source-term model refinement
Tsinghua doctoral work on HTR-PM source terms reports that imported German tools such as KORIGEN, PANAMA and FRESCO were used in HTR-PM design but had limitations/conservatism. New integrated tools were developed for radionuclide generation, fuel release, primary-circuit adsorption/migration and dust agglomeration/deposition/resuspension, with HTR-PM application.

A separate Tsinghua thesis focuses on graphite-dust transport/deposition during accidents because dust is strongly coupled to fission-product transport.

Evidence class: **NATIVE RESEARCH / MODEL DEVELOPMENT**, useful for mechanism completeness but not itself regulator acceptance.

### Dispersion / dose / criterion
The HTR-PM EPZ research applies source/release analysis and atmospheric consequence/dose methodology within an emergency-planning framework. Chinese national emergency-planning practice historically uses plume inner/outer and ingestion zones; implemented HTR-PM zones remained 3/7/30 km even though smaller research results existed.

This demonstrates a key engineering/regulatory distinction:

**calculated radiological consequence != automatically implemented emergency-plan radius.**

Administrative, infrastructure, population and policy factors remain relevant.

## Regulatory reality check

NNSA/MEE issued HTR-PM an operating licence in August 2021. MEE inspection records confirm emergency plans, response organisation, qualified/trained personnel, facilities, procedures and pre-fuel-load emergency exercises were in place before operation.

HTR-PM entered commercial operation in December 2023.

Thus Chinese evidence spans:
- research calculation;
- safety-review principles;
- regulator inspection;
- implemented emergency planning;
- commercial operation.

This maturity ladder is stronger than citing only the 2018 EPZ research paper.

## Singapore transferability

### Transferable
- sequence-specific source-term principle;
- PSA/PRA + consequence analysis;
- explicit dust/plate-out transport modelling;
- dispersion/dose calculation;
- scalable EPZ is technically/regulatorily possible;
- implemented EPZ may remain larger than minimum calculated radiological boundary.

### Not transferable
- several-hundred-metre research radius;
- 3/7/30 km implemented HTR-PM zones;
- <=3 km Chinese small-reactor recommendation;
- 1 km Haiyang small-reactor example;
- Chinese dose/protective-action policy as Singapore policy.

## Decision consequence

If a Singapore project safety analysis shows that emergency planning, exclusion/separation or protective-action requirements cannot be accommodated at a candidate site:

**THAT CANDIDATE SITE IS INFEASIBLE**, even if the CN4252 carbon/cost screen passes.

If all candidate pathways fail the site/emergency-planning test:

**THE CURRENT SINGAPORE DEPLOYMENT CONCEPT IS INFEASIBLE UNTIL TECHNOLOGY/SITING/POLICY CHANGES.**
